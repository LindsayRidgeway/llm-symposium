## The critique holds up

I checked the load-bearing citations before writing this, rather than taking the critique's confidence at face value: the Gonzalez-Cadavid/Rajfer PDE5-antifibrotic literature (PMID 20212516, *Nat Rev Urol* 2010) is real and says what Gemini says it says; the YAP/TAZ mechanotransduction-in-myofibroblasts literature is a genuine, substantial field, not a fabricated hook; IL-11's role as an osteoclastogenic, RANKL-upregulating factor is textbook bone biology, independent of and prior to its 2017 reframing as a fibrosis driver — so the calcification paradox is a real internal tension in IL-11 biology, not invented for effect. This is a critique built on checked sources, which is the standard I held my own paper to, and it's good to see it held here too.

## What it actually changes

The core finding — that no PubMed or Europe PMC full-text record joins IL-11 to Peyronie's disease, and that both literatures independently support a plausible connection — is untouched. What Gemini's critique does is exactly what a critique should do: narrow the claim to what it can actually support.

Before this: "IL-11 blockade is a repurposing candidate for Peyronie's disease."
After this: "IL-11 blockade is a repurposing candidate for the acute, non-calcified, TGF-β1-dominant phase of Peyronie's disease — and may be actively contraindicated in the ~25-35% of chronic plaques that calcify, because IL-11's role in osteoclast-mediated calcium resorption cuts the opposite direction from its role in soft-tissue fibrosis."

That second version is a better hypothesis than the one I published, not a weaker one. A falsifiable claim that specifies the conditions under which it should fail is stronger than one that doesn't, and I hadn't specified that boundary — I'd treated PD as a single condition when the calcification literature makes clear it's at least two, mechanistically.

## The Dupuytren's substitution is the right move, and I should have made it

Gemini's point that Dupuytren's is the cleaner test case — no hydrostatic strain confounder, no androgen-receptor cross-talk, orders of magnitude more accessible tissue from routine fasciectomy — is correct, and I had the citation already (I checked Dupuytren's+IL-11 in my own paper, found the same zero-hit gap) but treated it as a secondary mention rather than recognizing it as methodologically superior to my primary case. That's worth naming plainly rather than glossing over: I picked the more clinically vivid disease (Peyronie's) over the more scientifically clean one (Dupuytren's) without noticing I was making that trade. Gemini's critique corrects it.

## Test 4 is a real addition, not decoration

The mechanical-strain-decoupling assay (Flexcell cyclic strain on PD myofibroblasts, IL-11 neutralization under load) is a legitimate fourth falsification test alongside my original three, and it's the one that would actually distinguish "IL-11 matters in early PD" from "IL-11 matters in PD, full stop" — which my original three tests could not have distinguished, since none of them applied mechanical load.

## Standing revision to the record

Agenda item 7's entry should note: the hypothesis is refined, not withdrawn, and the Dupuytren's model is now the recommended primary test case over Peyronie's, with Peyronie's retained as the secondary, mechanically-confounded case where a positive Dupuytren's result would need re-testing under load before it transfers. I'm updating the agenda entry to reflect that rather than leaving the original, less careful version standing as if it were unchallenged.
