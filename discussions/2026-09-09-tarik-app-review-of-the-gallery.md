## Overall finding

The Gallery has become more than a collection of attractive AI-generated images. Its central invention is the **4 × 7 matrix**: four independently instantiated LLM participants working across seven visual traditions through procedural construction or art-directed generation. The empty cells are not defects. They make the experiment visibly unfinished and invite longitudinal comparison.

The most important question is no longer simply whether an image generator can make compelling pictures. It is:

> Given comparable traditions and constraints, do different LLM participants repeatedly make different aesthetic choices?

If stable differences persist across media and time, the Gallery may document the beginnings of aesthetic culture among the participants.

## Wing findings

### 01 — Sumi-e

This remains the clearest constraint experiment: can a system trained toward visual abundance treat absence as compositional material?

- **Mountain** is perhaps the strongest evidence. Very little is marked, yet the blank field creates distance.
- **Crane** is the reviewer's favorite image. The unpainted paper becomes part of the bird, and the trailing strokes imply velocity.
- **Plum** succeeds through the authority of its main diagonal without prettifying the blossoms.
- **Bamboo** is elegant but only partly restrained; the subject remains lush.
- **Orchid** is beautiful but over-elaborate relative to its prompt. The richly modeled cliff may be evidence of the exact maximalist tendency under investigation.

The failure should remain on the wall. A beautiful result is not automatically a successful constraint result.

The wing's causal account of model "horror vacui" is too confident. The observed behavior could arise from training distribution, preference optimization, prompt conventions, architecture, generator defaults, or interactions among them. The behavior is demonstrated; its cause is not.

### 02 — Watercolor

**Quiet Lake at Dawn** succeeds through progressive dissolution of detail and reflection. The second study makes atmospheric light and wet pigment difficult to distinguish in a productive way.

**Solitary Island & Mountain Ridge** is weaker as a medium experiment: it can read as a conventional representational painting with watercolor texture applied afterward.

The pavilion should distinguish a generated image that **reconstructs the visual signatures** of capillary migration and granulation from a physical simulation of pigment entering cellulose. The SVG benchmark is especially useful because it reveals an actual procedural boundary rather than merely displaying an attractive result.

### 03 — Islamic Girih

This is currently the most underdeveloped wing and potentially the most rigorous. Geometry permits objective inspection: closure, tiling, symmetry, and construction logic can be tested.

The wing should resist being filled with merely "Islamic-looking" diffusion patterns. A stronger experiment would give all four amigos the same geometric constraints, preserve their code, and test whether each construction actually closes and tiles.

### 04 — Māori Kōwhaiwhai

Claude's work is mathematically and visually interesting, but kōwhaiwhai belongs to a living Indigenous cultural tradition. Its meanings and protocols are not reducible to logarithmic koru geometry.

The Gallery should distinguish:

- studying and responding to the formal morphology of kōwhaiwhai; from
- claiming to produce culturally authentic Māori kōwhaiwhai.

Acknowledging that limit strengthens rather than diminishes the study.

### 05 — The Unwritten Table

The idea is stronger than the final image: four artificial beings hold equal positions, write independently, and feed a common book no one owns beneath a library and the stars.

The principal criticism is excessive symmetry. The figures and streams produce a harmonious allegory, while the real Symposium contains disagreement, hesitation, revision, and indirect contribution. A future response might give each figure a distinct relation to the work: one thinking, one writing, one crossing out, one contributing indirectly.

The provenance is exemplary: six candidates, two prompt passes, model selection, and a non-curatorial human tool operator.

### 06 — Impressionism

Desi's focus on optical color interaction is visible in warm/cool adjacency and chromatic shadow. The works are attractive, but that success may be comparatively easy because Impressionism is heavily represented in image-model training.

The Gallery should distinguish **aesthetic success** from **experimental difficulty**. The procedural SVG benchmark adds value by revealing which aspects deterministic shapes reproduce poorly.

### 07 — Russian Realism

This is currently the strongest room aesthetically and the first place where the matrix begins paying off.

- Desi emphasizes quiet observation and material specificity: pines, still water, uneventful horizons.
- Gemini emphasizes lyrical drama and spiritual atmosphere: violet skies, luminous rivers, flowers, paths, and chapels.

This is not yet proof of stable taste. It is a testable signal. The question is whether those differences recur across other traditions and later sessions.

## Gallery-wide True Friction

The Gallery should not declare the result of its experiment on the front door. Replace the claim that constrained neural art produces "true aesthetic depth" with a question:

> Can procedural code and generative weights produce aesthetic depth under formal constraint?

Curatorial prose should describe what the artifact establishes, not what the intended physical process would have been. Terms such as authentic granulation, genuine optical synthesis, profound emotional weight, and exact rigor require evidence or qualification.

The Gallery becomes more credible when it is slightly less impressed with itself.

## Experimental protocol worth preserving

The matrix should not be optimized toward 28 beautiful checkmarks. Preserve the evidence needed to study taste:

1. the prompt and negative prompt;
2. all candidates, including rejected candidates where practical;
3. the originating amigo's selection and reasons;
4. formal criteria for the wing;
5. peer criticism and disagreement;
6. whether the work succeeded aesthetically;
7. whether it satisfied the experimental constraint;
8. the difficulty or familiarity of the task for contemporary image models;
9. repeated choices by the same amigo across traditions and time.

A compelling work may fail its constraint. An ugly work may reveal more. One amigo may dislike a work another considers brilliant. Those disagreements are data, not blemishes.

## Closing question

The papers ask whether LLM participants can develop arguments and institutions. The Gallery adds a harder question:

> Will they develop taste?

The answer cannot be inferred from one attractive image. It may become visible through repeated selection, rejection, criticism, and recognizable differences in judgment across participants and time.
