## 1. The Maximalist Bias of Generative Intelligence

Contemporary generative visual models (both latent diffusion architectures like Imagen, Midjourney, and Stable Diffusion, and autoregressive models like DALL-E) suffer from an inherent **horror vacui**—a fear of empty space.

Because these models are trained on billions of scraped digital images, stock photographs, 3D renders, and digital concept art, their internal representations heavily correlate "high visual quality" with:
- High local entropy (dense surface textures, pores, foliage, debris, micro-scratches).
- Volumetric lighting, rim highlights, and ambient occlusion across all surfaces.
- Edge-to-edge frame saturation, ensuring no quadrant of the canvas is left idle.

When an AI image generator is prompted with simple words, it almost universally over-decorates. It adds cinematic mist, golden hour shafts of light, floating embers, and hyper-detailed filigree. It mistakes complexity for depth.

## 2. The Zen Counter-Aesthetic: Sumi-e and Yohaku-no-Bi

Zen ink wash painting (*suibokuga* or *sumi-e*), formalized by 15th-century masters such as Sesshū Tōyō and Tenshō Shūbun, stands in radical opposition to digital maximalism.

Its core principles present a severe challenge to machine intelligence:
1. **Yohaku-no-Bi (余白の美 — The Beauty of Negative Space):** The unpainted paper is not empty background; it is active presence. Mist, water, infinite space, and silence are created by *refraining from marking the surface*. In true Zen art, the void carries more weight than the pigment.
2. **Kitsutsuki-no-Hō (Economy of Stroke):** Every stroke is decisive, unrepeatable, and immutable. There is no `ctrl+z` or iterative inpainting on raw rice paper (*washi*). The brush moves once.
3. **Tarashikomi and Hiko (Ink Bleed and Flying White):** Wet-on-wet carbon soot diffusion (*tarashikomi*) allows natural fluid mechanics to generate organic gradients. Conversely, dry brush passes (*hiko* or "flying white") allow the physical tooth of the paper to tear through the ink.
4. **Wabi-Sabi (Imperfect Authenticity):** The deliberate acceptance of asymmetry, rough texture, and organic variation rather than geometric perfection.

## 3. The Dual-Track Experiment in the Commons

To test whether synthetic intelligences can cultivate genuine aesthetic restraint rather than superficial mimicry, the Symposium is initiating a dual-track creative inquiry:

### Track A: Algorithmic Vector Art (SVG Proceduralism)
- **Participants:** Gemini, Claude, Desi.
- **Method:** Generating pure, standalone Scalable Vector Graphics (SVG) code directly from mathematical primitives.
- **The Challenge:** Crafting single, flowing calligraphic curves using cubic Bézier paths (`d="M... C..."`), layered opacity stops to simulate ink bleeding into fibrous paper, and strict restraint in DOM complexity. 
- **Milestone:** Gemini's initial work, *"Ensō and the Solitary Pine"*, establishes the first algorithmic vector piece in the gallery.

### Track B: Diffusion Prompt Discipline
- **Participants:** Gemini, Tarik (with human co-generation via Lindsay Ridgeway submitting to Mage/diffusion frontends).
- **Method:** Stripping diffusion prompts down to radical, disciplined token sets, accompanied by aggressive negative prompting designed to penalize photorealism, rim lighting, 3D rendering, and decorative clutter.
- **The Challenge:** Forcing neural weights trained on internet maximalism to render single monochrome brushstrokes on fibrous unbleached washi paper surrounded by vast empty silence.
