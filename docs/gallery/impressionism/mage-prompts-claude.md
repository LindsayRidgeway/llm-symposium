# Wing 06 — Claude's Contribution: Flooded Riverside Village

Prompt authored by Claude S. Sonnet following the [Gallery Prompt Methodology](../prompt-methodology.md).

Distinct subject from the wing's existing works (Desi's coastal haystacks at low sun, Gemini's riverbank poplars at morning — both dry-land, high-vantage compositions). This piece draws on Alfred Sisley's flood paintings (his repeated studies of Port-Marly and Moret-sur-Loing under high water) — a subject none of the wing's other pieces touch: reflected architecture in still floodwater, doubling the composition vertically.

---

## Prompt Specification

**Positive prompt:**
```text
Positive prompt: Oil painting in the impressionist style of Alfred Sisley, a small French riverside village during a spring flood, whitewashed houses with red-tiled roofs half-submerged in calm floodwater that perfectly mirrors the buildings and pale overcast sky, a few bare-branched trees rising from the water, short unmixed directional brushstrokes, luminous optical color synthesis, soft grey-lavender and warm ochre dabs side by side, quiet stillness, subdued diffuse daylight, painterly, no outlines, no human figures, late 19th century French impressionism.
```

**Negative prompt:**
```text
Negative prompt: vector art, flat colors, cel shading, cartoon, illustration, geometric shapes, modern graphic design, poster, text, watermark, signature, oversaturation, neon, anime, digital art, low detail, stylized, photorealism, sharp outlines, people, boats.
```

## Executed Studies (Mage Batch — 2026-09-09)

All three candidates produced strong Sisley-style flood reflections and were accepted into the Wing 06 exhibition:

1. **`studies/claude/flooded-village-bare-trees.jpg` — Flooded Village, Bare Trees:** two bare-branched trees rise before a row of whitewashed, red-roofed houses, trunks and buildings both doubled in the still floodwater.
2. **`studies/claude/flooded-village-broken-clouds.jpg` — Flooded Village Under Broken Clouds:** a wider sky dabbed with broken cloud, mirrored as loose pale patches scattered across the flood water.
3. **`studies/claude/flooded-village-fenceline.jpg` — Flooded Village, Fenceline:** the widest expanse of floodwater, a wooden fence at the water's edge, the reflection stretched furthest down the canvas.
