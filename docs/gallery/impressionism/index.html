Exhibition Piece 01 • Optical Color Synthesis

Haystacks by the Sea at Dusk

Rooted in Claude Monet's Meules series, this study examines how neural diffusion models render the optical collision between late-afternoon golden warmth and cool marine shadows. Instead of blending pigments smoothly on a palette, unmixed strokes of warm cadmium orange and cool cobalt violet are placed in close proximity.

The vibrating atmosphere is created by light quantization: the eye fuses separate dabs of paint at distance, reproducing the genuine sensation of low-angle coastal sun reflecting off sea spray and straw.

Exhibition Piece 02 • Vertical Stroke Cadence

Poplars on the Epte at Morning

Following Claude Monet's celebrated 1891 Poplars series along the River Epte and Camille Pissarro's open-air field studies, this work investigates rapid, unmixed vertical brushwork under radiant mid-morning illumination. Slender poplars rise in rhythmic succession along the water's edge, their golden-green crowns shimmering against a pale summer sky.

The optical mixing is vivid: individual dashes of cadmium yellow sunlight, viridian green, cobalt blue sky reflections, and violet river shadow dabs sit side by side without software blending. The human retina synthesizes the trembling surface of the water and the gentle breeze moving through the canopy.

Exhibition Piece 03 • Doubled Verticals

Flooded Riverside Village

Distinct from the wing's haystacks and poplars, this work draws on Alfred Sisley's celebrated 1876 flood studies of Port-Marly and Moret-sur-Loing under high water.

Two bare-branched trees rise in front of a row of whitewashed, red-roofed village cottages, their trunks and architectural facades doubled in still floodwater beneath loose, broken cloud dabs.

Exhibition Piece 04 • Departed Action

After the Ferry Has Passed

The ferry is absent; its broken wake is the event. The suite tests whether separated warm and cool strokes can make a transient consequence—not a depicted object—organize an ordinary riverbank.

The widening broken wake remains central while leaning mooring posts frame rather than dominate. Warm and cool marks generate light without theatrical sunset.
