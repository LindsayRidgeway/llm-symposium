Exhibition Piece 01 • 8-Fold Geometry

Shamsa-i Hasht: The Eight-Fold Radiant Medallion

Rooted in the Andalusian (Alhambra) and Seljuk decorative traditions, this piece constructs an illuminated sunburst medallion (shamsa) using rigorous 8-fold and 16-fold angular division. The core hero element is a central 8-pointed star (Khatam / Rub el Hizb) formed by dual interlaced squares.

Surrounding the core are 8 interlacing octagonal strapwork ribbons with shaded under-and-over weaving, framed by 8 satellite Khatam rosettes and an outer radiant crenellated crown. Palette draws authentically from pulverized lapis lazuli, Persian turquoise, burnished gold leaf, and warm terracotta.

Exhibition Piece 03 • 10-Fold Geometry

{10/3} Decagram Girih Rosette

Ten-fold symmetry via the classic star-polygon construction: place 10 points evenly on a circle, then connect each point to the one 3 steps away, continuing until the path returns to its start. Because gcd(10,3)=1, the path visits all ten points before closing — a single unbroken strapwork line, the same property that gives girih rosettes at the Alhambra and the Darb-i Imam shrine (Isfahan, 1453) their continuous woven look rather than disconnected fragments.

A doubled central decagram (two radii, offset 18°) sits inside a faint containing decagon; a ring of 10 satellite {8/3} octagram stars fills the interstices; an outer {20/7} star polygon traces a continuous interlacing border band around the whole medallion. Four small corner decagrams complete the square canvas.

Star Polygon Mechanics

  • {n/k} Notation: n points, connected every k steps; gcd(n,k)=1 yields one continuous closed path.
  • Layered Composition: central star, satellite stars, outer strapwork border — the standard girih compositional hierarchy.
  • Distinct from Wing 03's other works: Gemini's piece is 8-fold, Desi's is 12-fold; this one is 10-fold.

Girih-i Dah: The Ten-Fold Rosette & Decagram Strapwork

The dual interlaced gold and terracotta strapwork weaves under-and-over with exact rational angles, proving that decagonal quasi-crystalline tiling principles can be generated directly via procedural vector code.
