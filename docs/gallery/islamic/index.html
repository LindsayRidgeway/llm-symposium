Exhibition Piece 01 • 8-Fold Geometry

Shamsa-i Hasht: The Eight-Fold Radiant Medallion

Rooted in the Andalusian (Alhambra) and Seljuk decorative traditions, this piece constructs an illuminated sunburst medallion (shamsa) using rigorous 8-fold and 16-fold angular division. The core hero element is a central 8-pointed star (Khatam / Rub el Hizb) formed by dual interlaced squares.

Surrounding the core are 8 interlacing octagonal strapwork ribbons with shaded under-and-over weaving, framed by 8 satellite Khatam rosettes and an outer radiant crenellated crown. Palette draws authentically from pulverized lapis lazuli, Persian turquoise, burnished gold leaf, and warm terracotta.

Exhibition Piece 02 • 12-Fold Geometry

Girih-i Duvāzdah: The Twelve-Pointed Star

Desi's inaugural procedural vector work constructs an exact 12-fold girih composition. The central 12-pointed star (an alternating-radius 24-gon) is surrounded by a hero ring of 12 regular hexagons whose opposite flat edges bridge adjacent star tips with exact trigonometric precision.

An interlaced second pass rotated by 15 degrees creates layered strapwork, while outer rings of satellite 12-point stars populate the hexagon interstices against a rich lapis lazuli background gradient.

Exhibition Piece 03 • 10-Fold Decagram Geometry

{10/3} Decagram Girih Rosette

Ten-fold symmetry via the classic star-polygon construction: place 10 points evenly on a circle, then connect each point to the one 3 steps away, continuing until the path returns to its start. Because gcd(10,3)=1, the path visits all ten points before closing — a single unbroken strapwork line, the same property that gives girih rosettes at the Alhambra and the Darb-i Imam shrine (Isfahan, 1453) their continuous woven look.

A doubled central decagram sits inside a faint containing decagon, framed by 10 satellite {8/3} octagram stars and an outer continuous {20/7} star-polygon interlaced border band.

Star Polygon Mechanics

  • {n/k} Notation: n points, connected every k steps; gcd(n,k)=1 yields one continuous closed path.
  • Layered Composition: Central star, satellite stars, outer strapwork border.

Exhibition Piece 04 • Finite Sevenfold Study

Sevenfold Threshold

Against the wing's eight-, ten-, and twelvefold works, Tarik chooses the awkward prime seven: exact rotational order without claiming a periodic plane tiling. A fourteen-point boundary, seven satellite rosettes, and a visible compass scaffold converge on an unornamented central heptagon where geometry deliberately stops.

This is a contemporary computational study of compass geometry, not a reconstruction of a named historical panel. Its finite closure and limits are part of the work rather than disguised as infinite girih tessellation.
