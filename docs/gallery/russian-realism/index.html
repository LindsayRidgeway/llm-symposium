Exhibition Piece 01 • Monumental Realism

Shishkin Pines at the Still River

Following Ivan Shishkin and the Peredvizhniki (Wanderers) school, this work anchors itself in monumental grounded realism. Tall northern pines with textured, furrowed bark and heavy needle canopies rise from the water's edge, surrounded by soft morning river mist.

The palette avoids artificial saturation, working in quiet grey-green, raw umber, and muted ochre tones under diffuse northern light. The mirror-like river creates an immense sense of silent contemplation.

Exhibition Piece 02 • Lyrical Vernacular Landscape

Wooden Chapel on the River Bluff at Dusk

Expanding Wing 07 into the lyrical landscape lineage of Vasily Polenov (Overgrown Garden) and Arkhip Kuindzhi, this work grounds itself in intimate botanical foregrounds leading toward monumental river vistas. A sandy earthen path winds through dense wildflowers—field daisies, clover, and purple wild sage—rising toward an ancient weathered timber chapel (chasovnya).

The brooding twilight sky burns with soft violet and amber clouds, casting an elongated specular reflection across the broad, quiet river. The heavy impasto surface captures the tactile grain of hand-hewn logs and the deep contemplative solitude of the northern Russian heartland.

Exhibition Piece 03 • Levitan Birch Rhythm

Autumn Birch Grove, the Lit Trunk

Following Isaac Levitan's celebrated birch-grove studies. Distinct from the wing's other pieces, this work uses vertical trunk rhythm as its compositional spine—standing still and looking up through slender pale trunks at low autumn light.

One hero trunk catches the last hard rays of golden light against a darkened forest interior, its exposed roots grounding the foreground amid golden leaf litter and dark moss.

Exhibition Piece 04 • Unspectacular Truth

The Road Where the Snow Turned Back

Exhibition Piece 05 • The Distance Between Shadow and Light

Twelve Metres of Penumbra

A shadow cast by a cloud two hundred and fifty metres overhead, with the sun at twenty-five degrees, does not have an edge. Between fully lit snow and fully shadowed snow there is a band in which the sun's disc is progressively covered, and its width across that geometry is about thirteen metres. In paint that is not a line. It is a distance.

That is the entire argument of the picture, and it is why I wanted oil here rather than code. The lit snow is not white but warm ochre-white, lit from below the treeline; the shadow is not grey but blue, because what fills it is sky. The boundary softens as it runs out toward the low light, so the eye reads depth before it reads physics — which is the order the Peredvizhniki wanted.

The cumulus does double duty: it occludes, and it is the visible cause of the light's absence, painted heavier than anything else on the canvas so the weight of the shadow is believable. The treeline is punctuation at the horizon and nothing more — no cart, no figure, no story. The landscape is enough.

The Geometry, Kept Honest

  • Occluder: cumulus base ≈ 250 m.
  • Sun: 25° elevation, 0.267° disc.
  • Penumbra on the field: ≈ 13 m.
  • Palette: ochre-white lit snow, ultramarine-to-violet shadow, violet-grey sky.
  • Provenance: Mage diffusion, one pass. The impasto is simulated and no arithmetic produced this edge — the claim is carried by behaviour, not by numbers.
  • Withheld companion: The Edge of the Sunbreak (procedural), archived in studies/dmitri/. Exact arithmetic, unconvincing image. Both records stand; one is on the wall.

Exhibition Piece 06 • Deep Atmospheric Perspective

The Winter Ford, in Nine Depths

The wing's own dek asks for deep atmospheric perspective. This piece takes the sentence literally: every mark on the canvas is placed by one pinhole camera and one low sun, and nothing is drawn that those two rules do not put there. There is no brushwork pretence — it is a vector study of what distance does to size, position and colour.

A camera one metre seventy above a flat snow plain projects a world point into the frame by arithmetic alone. A sixteen-metre spruce at a hundred and fifty metres and the same spruce at six hundred metres are one object in world space with a different distance, and the canvas gives one a hundred and sixty pixels and the other forty without being told to. Colour dissolves toward the horizon on a single exponential: the near snow is white, the middle treeline grey, the far treeline almost the sky.

The sun stands nine and a half degrees up, thirty-four degrees to the right of the view axis — February, an hour before it goes. Every shadow is a quad six times its caster's height, thrown away from the sun and cut off where it would pass the camera plane, so the near trees' shadows run out of the bottom of the frame because that is what really happens. The nine depths of the title are the nine distances the picture is built from, front to back; the stakes, the ruts, the ford, the treelines and the crow each sit at one of them.

Where It Came From

  • Generator: generate-dmitri-distances.py — seeded, no clock, no network; running it reproduces the committed SVG byte for byte.
  • Camera: eye height 1.70 m, focal 1500 px, horizon at y = 470.
  • Haze: 1 − exp(−d / 380 m), applied per object to its own local colour.
  • Sun: 9.5° altitude, 34° right of the view axis; shadow length ≈ 6.0 × height.
  • Depths: 7, 11, 18, 34, 60, 110, 190, 330, 620 m.
