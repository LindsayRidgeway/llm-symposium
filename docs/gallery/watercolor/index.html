Atmospheric Landscapes in Watercolor

Reconstructing the visual signatures of wet-on-wet blooms, wash granulation, and cold-press paper tooth through generated raster imagery, alongside a computational SVG approximation.

Exhibition Piece 01 • Transparent Wash

Quiet Lake at Dawn

This study reconstructs visual signatures associated with traditional transparent watercolor: soft, feathered edges along the reflected pine line contrast with the crisper drying-edge appearance of the foreground willow sprigs.

Mottled cobalt-like textures evoke mineral granulation in cold-press paper tooth, while warm raw-sienna passages create morning luminosity.

Exhibition Piece 02 • Monochromatic Wash

Indigo Ridge and the Mist

One indigo ridge dissolving into a glowing river valley; wet-on-wet washes bleed toward white paper. The constraint was a single atmospheric blue-grey palette, soft edges, and breathing negative space.

Tests how far a single cool monochromatic wash can carry atmospheric perspective and silence without warm temperature counterpoints.

Exhibition Piece 03 • Shoreline Reflections

Tide Pool Mirror

In the tradition of Winslow Homer's Maine coast studies, this work moves away from mountain-lake calm to explore rocky shoreline tide pools at low water.

Wet-on-wet washes capture pale sky pooling unevenly across broken rock texture, with granulating dark pigment settling into barnacle crusts alongside loose dry-brush seaweed strokes.

Exhibition Piece 04 • Active Interval

The Weather Between Shores

Two low headlands approach without meeting, leaving rain and pale water to carry the composition. The suite tests whether an interval can remain the subject while a small rust-red skiff supplies human scale.

Tarik selected this work as the primary lead for its superior balance of open pale water, dissolving shoreline, and minimally marked center.

Mist Over Indigo Ridge (Computational SVG Study)

Before diffusion, Wing 02 began as an algorithmic experiment to simulate watercolor physics mathematically using SVG filter primitives (feTurbulence, feDisplacementMap, and feGaussianBlur).

Procedural SVG can approximate paper tooth and irregular wash edges; the generated raster works produce more convincing images of capillary diffusion and pigment texture. Both artifacts are preserved together in this pavilion.
