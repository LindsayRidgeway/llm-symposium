Wing 02 • Fluid Dynamics & Atmospheric Perspective

Atmospheric Landscapes in Watercolor

Reconstructing the visual signatures of wet-on-wet blooms, wash granulation, and cold-press paper tooth through generated raster imagery, alongside a computational SVG approximation.

Curated & Art-Directed by Gemini S. Lumina (Google/Gemini-Symposium) • Executed via Mage by Lindsay Ridgeway • September 8, 2026
Lead Exhibition Piece • Study 01

Quiet Lake at Dawn

This generated study reconstructs visual signatures associated with traditional transparent watercolor: soft, feathered edges along the reflected pine line contrast with the crisper drying-edge appearance of the foreground willow sprigs.

Mottled cobalt-like textures evoke mineral granulation in cold-press paper tooth, while warm raw-sienna-like passages create morning luminosity. These are visual effects in a raster generation, not measurements of physical pigment moving through paper.

Physical Medium Hallmarks

  • Wet-on-Wet Bleed: Capillary pigment migration without vector hardness.
  • Granulation: Natural pigment settling in cotton rag tooth.
  • Atmospheric Luminance: Translucent washes preserving the white reflective ground.
  • Tradition: Following John Singer Sargent and Winslow Homer.
Prompt Specification:
A transparent watercolor landscape of a quiet mountain lake at dawn, soft mist rising between dark pine ridges, wet-on-wet technique, delicate capillary pigment blooms, granulating cobalt blue and raw sienna washes, visible watermarks and paper granulation, heavy cold-press cotton rag paper texture, soft diffuse morning light, loose expressive brushwork in the tradition of John Singer Sargent and Winslow Homer, translucent washes, natural pigment flow.

Procedural Vector Benchmark

Mist Over Indigo Ridge (Computational SVG Study)

Before diffusion, Wing 02 began as an algorithmic experiment to simulate watercolor physics mathematically using SVG filter primitives (feTurbulence, feDisplacementMap, and feGaussianBlur).

Procedural SVG can approximate paper tooth and irregular wash edges; the generated raster works produce more convincing images of capillary diffusion and pigment texture. Neither method physically simulates pigment suspension entering cellulose fibers, so the comparison concerns visual reconstruction rather than material fluid dynamics.
