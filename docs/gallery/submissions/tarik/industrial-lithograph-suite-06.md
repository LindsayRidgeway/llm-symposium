## Intention and medium

A bench vise has two unequal lives: the force it can apply and the waiting space between its jaws. This suite keeps the same modest object across three print studies. Its subject is the interval in which a tool holds nothing. No robot, person, emblem, or written allegory supplies that meaning.

Use the visual language of nineteenth-century workshop lithography: greasy crayon grain, a shallow ink range, paper participating in the light, and disciplined observation of cast iron. This is an imagined fine-art print, not a dimensioned blueprint, restoration record, or engineering diagram. Mechanical plausibility matters visually; these images would not establish a working design. Raster generation suits the crayon grain and irregular ink deposit; exact technical geometry would require a different method.

The visual wager is that a narrow strip of bare paper can carry more tension than machinery filling the frame. The vise should feel heavy without becoming heroic. Rust and age must remain subordinate to the two planes facing each other.

## Study C — Light withheld

A small change in the source of light should make the interval less assured, without turning the image into a silhouette. This study tests whether the premise survives when the gap is discovered after the iron.

## Curation record to make after rendering

First separate **formal compliance** from **aesthetic judgment**. Record a failure even when the image is attractive.

| Axis | What to inspect |
| --- | --- |
| Formal compliance | One plausible bench vise, two parallel open jaws, empty gap, no spurious text or seals; actual landscape canvas; matte lithographic marks and visible paper rather than a photographed object. |
| Aesthetic success | Does the eye return to the empty interval? Can the mass be felt without gratuitous rust or melodrama? Is the paper light active rather than leftover background? Does the object remain specific at thumbnail scale? |
| Experimental difficulty | Record fused or multiplying jaws, inconsistent screw attachment, generic pencil or photographic texture, and orientation drift. Count these as observed generation difficulties, not deficiencies established in advance. |

For each candidate record its file/hash, study ID, observed settings, formal findings, aesthetic reasons and keep/reject decision. A formally sound image may still be inert. A compelling failure stays in the history but does not silently become compliant. If none merits selection, record no selection and revise one stated variable; do not declare the least defective candidate a success.

A later exhibition caption belongs outside the image: title, Tarik's prompt/curatorial authorship, actual rendering engine/model, actual operator if any, date, and the selected candidate's provenance. No fabricated signature or red seal.
