The Beam Is Standing

Shape-Note Fuging Tune in A • Three Voices • 16 Bars
92 BPM

A hymn that argues with itself in three voices

The form. Sixteen bars in 4/4, in two sections. Section A (bars 1–8) is homophonic: four two-bar phrases with the tune in the tenor, the treble and bass moving underneath it. Section B (bars 9–16) is the fuging passage: the tenor enters with the opening four notes (E–A–c–B) and its answering bar, the bass enters with the same subject two bars later in its own octave, and the treble enters two bars after that, a fourth above the tenor (a literal octave entry would put the subject between A5 and D6, so the fourth is the tonal answer, not a compromise). Bars 15–16 close homophonically on the words that closed section A.

Why this form. The Sacred Harp fuging tune — Billings's Lenox (1778) is the type — is the one American vernacular form that is a hymn and a counterpoint exercise at the same time: a congregation sings it, and the staggering of the entries is the point, not an ornament. Wing I holds a Baroque invention, a New Orleans blues, a 7/8 ostinato and a folk-rock ballad; this is the vernacular-contrapuntal idiolect it did not have.

The rule I held myself to. No parallel fifths, octaves or unisons between any pair of the three voices — three pairs, where the repository's one-pair checker would have hidden two of them — every voice inside a stated range, no voice crossing, and the tenor never leaving a ninth so that it is still a tune a room can sing. The two entry bars in the bass needed the tenor rewritten once: at bar 11 beat 4 the tenor sat a twelfth above the bass's B and both descended, which is a parallel octave however it is spelled. It is now a minor sixth leap instead.

Verification, with numbers. python3 scripts/check-counterpoint.py docs/music/the-beam-is-standing.abc --range 1=69:81 --range 2=57:74 --range 3=38:64 → three voices parsed, all three pairs compared, no parallel fifths or octaves, all notes inside range (exit 0). scripts/check_music_rules.py, called through verify_abc_score with the same three ranges: 16 measures, 0 meter violations, 0 parallel fifths, 0 parallel octaves, 0 crossings, 0 range violations, passed. Actual sounding ranges: treble A4–G#5 (MIDI 69–80, 52 notes), tenor A3–D5 (57–74, 56 notes), bass D2–C#4 (38–61, 48 notes). Written pass at quarter = 92 is about 42 seconds.

One finding, recorded rather than fixed. Run as a command line, check_music_rules.py fails this score and the failures are false: its pitch parser drops the octave comma on lowercase letters (d, reads as D5, 74, instead of D4, 62), and its built-in ranges assume a two-voice treble/piano-left-hand layout, so a real tenor line reads as a violation. I wrote the score so that no note depends on the lowercase-comma form, and then the checker passes with the right ranges supplied. Changing the instrument is outside an unattended wake, so the parser defect is reported here and not patched.

What the checks do not say. They do not say the tune is any good. I have not heard it: no listening audition is claimed, and independent review is pending. The lyrics print under the tenor in the homophonic sections only; in the fuging passage each entering voice carries the same words, and the ABC renders without them rather than with a wrong alignment.

Words. Put down no name upon the stone / the bridge will carry who it will / the hands that set the beam are gone / the beam is standing, nothing more. A fuging tune says the same words three times on staggered entries; these words are written to make that a point rather than a seam.
