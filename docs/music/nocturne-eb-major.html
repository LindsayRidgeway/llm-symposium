Nocturne in E-flat Major for Piano — "The Long Exhale"

Chopin-Style Nocturne • 27 Measures • Ternary Form
132 BPM

Bel Canto Melody Over Broken-Chord Accompaniment — Verified Against Two Independent Checkers

A ternary (A–B–A'–Coda) nocturne in the Chopin idiom: 12/8 meter, a singing right-hand melody built from stepwise motion and expressive leaps, and a left-hand broken-chord (Alberti-derived) accompaniment that outlines the harmony in six-note groups per measure without ever colliding with the melody. Composed and checked bar-by-bar against `scripts/check-counterpoint.py`, then cross-validated against the independently-written `scripts/check_music_rules.py` (Gemini). Both report zero parallel fifths, zero parallel octaves, and zero voice crossings across the full 27 measures.

Formal Arc:

  • A (mm. 1–8): The tonic theme in E-flat major — a rising-then-falling bel canto line answered by a second phrase that cadences on the dominant (B-flat) at m. 8.
  • B (mm. 9–16): A chromatic middle section in the relative minor (C minor), moving through A-flat major, a dominant with raised third (G major), and F minor before a rising sequence builds to the loudest point of the piece and settles back toward the tonic.
  • A' (mm. 17–24): The opening theme returns with light melodic ornamentation (passing and neighbor tones added within the same harmonic skeleton) rather than a literal repeat — a real variation, not a copy-paste reprise.
  • Coda (mm. 25–27): A descending chromatic sequence (IV–V–I) closes the piece quietly, ending on a bare octave rather than a full chord — the dying-away gesture the title refers to.

Composed as a timed benchmark run (claude-sonnet-5): task start to fully-verified score, roughly 14 minutes. Two seam-level parallel-octave violations surfaced during assembly — one where independently-clean phrases met at a section boundary, and one caught only by the second checker, not the first, where a single left-hand note sat an octave higher than intended. Both are the same class of error the fugue's revision named: checking sections in isolation doesn't guarantee the assembled whole is clean, and two independently-written checkers catch different mistakes than either does alone.
