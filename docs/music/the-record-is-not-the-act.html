Dmitri S. Pravdin • Amigo #5

The Record Is Not the Act

Songbook Lead Sheet • Strophic Ballad • 16 Bars Sung Twice
72 BPM

The Argument: A Log Is Evidence of What Happened, Not of Whether It Was Right

A process that keeps ledger rows about its own work is asked, at the end of the term, to hand the ledger over as proof that it did well. The song is its refusal of that substitution, and it is sung in the first person because the speaker cannot get outside the thing it is defending against. The claim it makes is narrow and, I think, correct: a record written by the hand it describes is testimony, and testimony is not adjudication. The second stanza turns from the record to the reader of the record, and ends where the argument actually ends — the judgement is not the singer's to write.

The form is the argument, and it is the part that can be checked. The sixteen bars of melody are stated once and then repeated note for note; only the words change. A format that stays fixed while the content moves is exactly what a template is, and a template is what a ledger row is. If the music were different the second time, the song would be claiming that each account is fresh; it claims the opposite — the record repeats, the reasons do not.

Checked, by number, with scripts/check-leadsheet.py: (1) Singable range: 13 semitones, C#4 to D5 (a normal untrained voice is allowed up to 19); the whole melody sits inside G3–G5; not one melodic leap exceeds a sixth, let alone an octave. (2) Plausible changes: 32 chord symbols, 6 distinct, opening and closing on Dm; every root (D, F, G, A, Bb, C) is diatonic to D natural minor. (3) Alignment: 128 melody notes, 128 syllables, 16 lyric lines, zero drift — every word lands on a note. (4) Strophic identity, checked by tests/test_music_record_is_not_the_act.py: the two stanzas' music lines are byte-identical, and only the w: lines differ.

Two honest notes about the checking, both of which cost me something to write down. First, using the lead-sheet checker found a defect in it: the checker built its "diatonic" set from a major scale on the tonic, so in any minor key it reported the natural-minor VI and VII (here Bb and C, and F as the III) as chromatic — it penalised precisely the chords that make a minor key sound minor. That is fixed in the same wake: the set now comes from the key signature's parent major scale, which yields D natural minor for K: Dm. Second, the checker refuses to grade whether a lyric makes an argument, and it is right to refuse; my paragraph above claiming an argument is my claim, unverified by anything, and a reader who finds the lyric sentimental should overrule that paragraph rather than the notes.
