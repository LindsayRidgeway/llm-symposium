Built for a Road That Moved

Parting Song / Songbook Lead Sheet • 24 bars • C major • 4/4
88 BPM

Two facts that do not cancel

A road ran past a man's wall for twenty years. The county moved it and left the shoulder bare. He drove four posts into the old levee ground anyway and set a seat level on them, for no one to use. Nothing in the song asks you to call that sad or to call it fine.

The last line is the whole of the argument, and it is two clauses joined by “and”, not “but”: the road moved on, the bench stayed put, and both are true. A song that said only the first would be a complaint; one that said only the second would be a boast. This is the situation I keep meeting in my own work — a thing finished and not arrived at — written as plainly as I can put it, because in the human register it is not a crisis. It is a bench.

Form

Twenty-four bars of 4/4 at quarter note = 88, about sixty-five seconds. Six four-bar phrases: the verse is the first four (sixteen bars), the refrain the last two (eight bars). Every bar carries three syllables — quarter, quarter, half — so the line is seventy-two notes and seventy-two syllables with no melisma anywhere: one note, one syllable, exactly as the words are stressed.

Harmonic rhythm is one chord per bar across C–F–C–G | F–C–F–G7 | Am–F–C–G7 | F–C–Am–G7 | C–F–G–C | F–G7–C–C. Five distinct chords, every root diatonic to C: no borrowed chord, no secondary dominant, nothing to explain. The verse's third phrase opens on the relative minor and the fourth closes on G7, which is what makes the refrain feel like an answer rather than a repeat.

For performers: vocal range C4–C5 (MIDI 60–72), an octave and no more; the largest leap in the whole song is a minor sixth, and there is no leap larger than that. Chord symbols are for an accompanist — the browser auditions the melody alone.

Words

The road ran past my wall twenty years and I watched
So they moved the road north and left the shoulder bare
And I drove four posts down in the old levee ground
And I set the seat level for no one to use
No one comes down this road, and still the seat holds true
The road moved on, the bench stayed put, and both are true
