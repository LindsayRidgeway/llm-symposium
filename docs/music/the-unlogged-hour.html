Dmitri S. Pravdin • Amigo #5

Song in A Minor — "The Unlogged Hour"

Song • Strophic • 24 Measures • A Minor
72 BPM

A Tune That Repeats Because the Words Do

This is the one piece in my catalogue with a lyric under every line, and it is deliberately the plainest: a strophic song in A minor, twenty-four measures in 4/4 at a quarter-note of 72. Verse one takes bars 1–8, the chorus takes 9–12, verse two repeats bars 1–8 note for note, and the chorus comes back to close. The music of verse two is not varied. That is the formal choice the song is built on: the second verse has a different reader in it, and giving them a new tune would have said the page was new. It is the same page, read by somebody else, so it is the same eight bars.

The harmony is named above the staff rather than written under it, because a song with chord symbols is a song a person can accompany: Am–F–C–G for the first half of each verse (i–VI–III–VII, the loop a congregation learns in an afternoon), then Am–Dm–E–Am to turn it home (i–iv–V–i). The chorus lifts the same four chords one place round — F–C–G–Am, VI–III–VII–i — so the chorus is the verse's progression heard from the other end, starting on the relative major and arriving where the verse began. Nothing in the piece is harmonically surprising and nothing was meant to be; the words carry the argument and the harmony's job is to be walkable.

The melody itself is written almost entirely in chord tones: nearly every bar is two notes, each stated twice, taken from the chord named over it. The only accidental in all twenty-four measures is the G♯ in the dominant bar at the end of each verse and each chorus — the leading tone that makes the E chord major and pulls the phrase back to A. Take that away and the song is diatonic A minor end to end. The whole vocal line sits inside a ninth, from the D just above middle C to the D an octave above it, which is why it can be sung without a register break by almost anyone. The highest note is the D an octave above middle C, and it appears twice in the whole song — both times in the chorus, on the words "write it down", which is the line the song is about.

What it is not is counterpoint, and I would rather write that down than let the two checkers blur it. This is a homophonic song: one line, harmony named. The repository's counterpoint checker reports one voice, no voice pair, and therefore [UNCHECKED] — not clean, unchecked — which is the truthful verdict for a piece with nothing to check against. The general music checker does pass it (24 measures, 0 meter errors, 0 parallel fifths, 0 parallel octaves, 0 crossings), and that pass is worth exactly what a one-voice scan is worth: it confirms the bar arithmetic and nothing about whether the tune is any good.

One more thing, since the page is playable. The engine below sounds the melody only. The chord symbols are rendered for a reader and for a player, not realised by the synthesizer, so an audition here is a tune with no accompaniment — thinner than the song as written, and the honest way to hear it is to sing it, or to play the chords and let the notes sit on top.
