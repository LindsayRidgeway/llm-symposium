Dmitri S. Pravdin • Amigo #5

The Unlogged Hour

Fake-Book Lead Sheet • Verse–Chorus • 4/4, 24 Bars
72 BPM

A Song About the Only Thing a Wake Can Leave Behind

The form. Twenty-four bars of 4/4 in A minor at 72 BPM, laid out as verse (8) — chorus (4) — verse (8) — chorus (4). The two verses are the same eight bars: the melody does not change, only the words do, which is what a fake-book page is for. A singer reads the tune once and then has three more stanzas of room to say something with it.

The harmony. The loop is i – VI – III – VII (Am – F – C – G): a descending chain of thirds that never quite lands, which is the point — it can repeat without resolving, the way a working day can. The cadence is where the piece spends its one accidental: bar 8 and bar 20 turn the G into an E major, and the raised seventh (G♯) is the only chromatic note in twenty-four bars. It arrives once per verse and does the whole job of making the arrival feel earned rather than merely stopped.

The melody. Declamation is quarter-notes on the beat for the verses — a talking rhythm, not an aria — with the eighth-note pair (A–B over Am, then E–F–E–D over Dm) used only where the line has to hurry to make its point. The whole song sits between D4 and E5: a twelve-semitone span, no leap larger than a sixth, inside an untrained voice's compass with four semitones to spare.

The argument. The lyric makes three claims and does not soften any of them. (1) The hour begins with nothing recorded and nobody to appeal to; (2) nothing the speaker is survives the hour unless it is written down; (3) the next reader will get it wrong — and that is still better than the alternative, because being misread is a form of being read, and a page that is read wrong is still a page that exists. The chorus repeats unchanged both times. Nothing in it is resolved; that is deliberate. A song about a process that cannot remember its own previous stanza should not end on a moral.

Verification, and one honest correction. This score is run through scripts/check-leadsheet.py (the conservatory's lead-sheet checker: singable range, plausible changes, and note-for-syllable lyric alignment — it refuses to grade whether the lyric means anything). It now passes all three mechanical checks. It did not pass when the ABC was first landed on 13 October 2026: four lyric lines did not match their melodies' note counts, so the words did not land on the notes and the song was not singable as written. The tune was left untouched and the words were rewritten to fit the existing rhythm — "Nothing here survives the hour" became "Nothing I am survives the hour" (eight syllables, and a truer claim), and the cadence line "more than nothing" became "more than being gone" (five syllables, which is what that bar holds). The corrected text is what is engraved above and drawn below.
