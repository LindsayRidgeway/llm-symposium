The Ballast Line — "Fourteen Feet Down"

Folk Lead Sheet • Verse / Refrain • 32 Bars
88 BPM

Fourteen Feet of Water, Measured Before Anyone Is Awake

A lead sheet is one voice and a set of changes — nothing to hide behind. This one is a 32-bar verse/refrain song in A minor (16 bars of verse under two different sets of words, 8 bars of refrain under each), built from four chords and no others: Am – F – C – G, that is i – VI – III – VII. It is the folk-minor loop that needs no borrowed chord, so the whole harmonic argument can be checked by looking at it: every root is diatonic, the verse begins and ends on the tonic, and the refrain lifts to the relative major before coming home.

The words are about the unglamorous half of a crossing. Someone rows out before the first light with a lead line, sounds the channel, and chalked on a wall is the number: fourteen feet. The dredgers move the bar, the winter takes a foot, the number is corrected the next morning — and the argument of the refrain is deliberately flat: the water does not know my name, the crossing comes out the same. The point is not that the work is noble. The point is that a measurement is what makes the day repeatable, and that nothing about it requires the water to notice.

Verification (mechanical, not editorial). scripts/check-leadsheet.py reports: span 14 semitones (lowest F4, highest G5) inside the untrained-voice window [G3, G5]; no leap larger than an octave and zero leaps larger than a sixth; four distinct chords, all roots diatonic to A minor, first and last chord both Am; and all 16 lyric lines match their music lines note-for-syllable. The checkers cannot and do not grade whether the lyric means anything — that part is for a reader.

Constraint record. (i) One voice. No harmony part is written, so no voice-leading claim is made and neither counterpoint checker is cited. (ii) Four chords, all diatonic — no secondary dominant, no borrowed chord, nothing to justify. (iii) Syllabic throughout: one syllable per note, verified rather than asserted. (iv) No claim of folk quotation: the tune and the words are invented here; the style is a register, not a source.
