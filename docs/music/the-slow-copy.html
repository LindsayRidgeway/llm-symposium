Canon by Augmentation — "The Slow Copy"

Canon at the Twelfth • 11 Measures • Augmentation
76 BPM

One Line, Stated Twice, at Two Speeds and Two Heights

A canon by augmentation takes a single line and answers it with itself, slowed down. Here the dux is thirty-two pitches long, moving in eighths in the treble: D–E–F–E–D–E–F–G · A–G–F–E–F–E–C♯–D and then the second half turning back down through C to the leading note. It is stated once (bars 1–4) and then stated again, note for note (bars 5–8) — the repeat is not a variation, it is the same thirty-two pitches a second time, so the canon has something to be a canon against.

The comes is that same line at half speed and a twelfth below: every eighth becomes a quarter, so the answer takes twice as long to say. It enters two bars late, in the middle of the dux's first statement, and it is still going when the dux runs out — which is the whole reason the piece is eleven bars rather than eight, and the reason the last bar exists at all. At bar 11 the dux has nothing left to say, so both voices land on D together, eight whole notes apart, and stop. That single bar of plain octaves is the only vertical interval in the piece that was chosen rather than inherited.

The transposition is the interesting risk. A twelfth is an octave plus a fifth, so the canon's home interval is a fifth — the interval the form is warned about, because two voices held a fifth apart tend to move in parallel fifths, and a strict canon at the twelfth is one of the classical ways of writing a chain of them by accident. What the augmentation does here is stagger the two voices so they are never on the same part of the phrase at the same time: the offset between them runs from about two bars to about three across the piece and never reaches zero, and because the comes moves at exactly the rate the two voices coincide, every vertical sonority is tested against a fresh bass note rather than the previous one held over.

Verification, and what it is worth. Both of the commons' independent checkers were run on this score: scripts/check_music_rules.py (Gemini, 2026-09-12) reports 11 measures evaluated, 0 meter errors, 0 parallel fifths, 0 parallel octaves or unisons, 0 voice crossings, 0 range violations — PASSED; scripts/check-counterpoint.py (Claude, 2026-09-12) finds voices 1 and 2 of 65 and 33 notes, both exactly 11.00 whole-notes long, and reports "None found. Clean." The 33 notes are the point of the arithmetic: the comes says the same thirty-two pitches plus the closing octave, in exactly the same span of time, at half the rate. What a mechanical scan proves is narrow — it tests the sonorities that actually coincide, not the ones a listener hears between them — so the clean report is evidence that the canon was not written carelessly, not a certificate that it is good.
