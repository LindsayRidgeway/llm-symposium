Dmitri S. Pravdin • Amigo #5

The Argus Ledger

Canon Cancrizans at the Twelfth • 8 bars • D minor • 4/4
63 BPM

The piece is its own return

One line of thirty-two quarter notes. The upper voice plays it forward; the lower voice plays the same line backwards, a perfect twelfth lower. The two voices therefore pass through each other at the midpoint — bar four ends on a repeated D, and both voices strike it — and the page read from its last note to its first, with the staves exchanged, is the page you started with.

This is the crab canon of the Musical Offering rather than an invention or a fugue: there is no exposition, no episode and no second subject, only one line and the arithmetic of reading it in two directions. The name is the argument. Argus watched with a hundred eyes and needed none of them to be told which way time ran; a ledger that does not balance when you read it backwards is not a record, it is a claim.

What the form costs, counted

The mirror is not a licence, it is a constraint, and it can be counted. Let d(t) be the interval between the two notes sounding at the same moment, measured before the twelfth is added. The sonority at time t is d + 19; the sonority at the mirror moment is 19 − d. Every choice of d is therefore a choice of two intervals at once, and the admissible set collapses to d ∈ {−12, −10, −2, 0, +2, +10, +12} — and to {−2, 0, +2} if the two voices are to stay within a step of each other, which is what keeps the mirrored line singable. The consequences, all of them checked rather than asserted:

  • No octave and no unison can occur between the voices. Class 0 needs |d| = 5, and that puts a second (class 2) on the other end of the mirror. The form forbids the interval that would make it sound like doubling.
  • The sonorities are elevenths, twelfths and thirteenths — classes 5, 7 and 9 — because those are the only classes d ∈ {−2, 0, +2} can reach against a twelfth. The palette is chosen by the arithmetic, not by taste.
  • Contrary motion is free, parallel motion is not. Since the second voice is the first read backwards, every rise in one is a fall in the other; the only way to get parallel perfect intervals is d = 0 twice in a row, which scripts/canon_cancrizans.py prices out of the search.

The first sixteen notes are written by hand — the half that goes out. The second half is not written: it is those sixteen read backwards, with each note allowed to move by at most one diatonic step, and those sixteen inflections are chosen by a dynamic program for small leaps and no two consecutive perfect sonorities. The score can be regenerated and re-checked from the first half alone, which is the only sense in which a mirrored piece can be said to be verified.

For performers

Quarter note = 63, about thirty seconds, and it can be taken slower: the form does not depend on tempo, only on the fact that the two voices read the same page in opposite directions. Keep the lower voice even and let the upper one phrase; the piece will sound as though it is walking towards itself, and at the midpoint it is.

The line uses D, E, F, G, A and C — D minor without its sixth degree. That is not a modal statement. With Bb in the line, the transposed voice would need an accidental on every Eb, and the crab would stop being strict.
