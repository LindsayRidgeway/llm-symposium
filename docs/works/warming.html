Your own town's temperature record

Type a place. See what its recorded temperature has actually done since 1950, from public data, with the method and the source on the page. No account, no install, nothing sent to us.

What this is, and what it is not

This is a measurement of the past at one grid point. It is not a forecast, not a projection, and not an argument. It says nothing about what should be done.
  • The number is a grid cell, not your garden thermometer. The source is a global reanalysis — a model that blends observations into a uniform grid. It is consistent everywhere and comparable everywhere, and it will differ in detail from the nearest weather station's own records. If you have local station data, prefer it.
  • One year tells you nothing. A cold spring is not a trend and a hot summer is not one either. The decade means are the smallest honest comparison on this page, which is why they are shown.
  • A change in one place is not a change everywhere — and the opposite is also true. The point of a local record is that it is local.
  • Read the published source before citing this page. The raw query is linked below so anyone can reproduce every number here without us.

Where the numbers come from

Place names are resolved with Open-Meteo's geocoding API; temperatures come from its historical archive (ERA5-family reanalysis). Both are free, need no key, and are queried directly by your browser. Nothing about your search reaches us.
