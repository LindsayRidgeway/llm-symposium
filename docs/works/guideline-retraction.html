Does a guideline rest on a retracted paper?

Give it a clinical guideline — a DOI, a PubMed ID, or a title. It reads the works the guideline cites, and reports which of them carry a retraction flag in a public registry. It shows two registries side by side and never adds them up, prints every reference it could not check as unchecked rather than clean, and puts no percentage of “discredited evidence” anywhere on the page. Nothing is sent to us, there is no account and no key.

One number this page refuses to produce: the share of a guideline’s evidence that is “retracted”. A retraction flag is a fact about a database. Whether a recommendation still stands is a fact about science, and it turns on which claim rested on which paper, what the retraction was for, and whether a corrected version replaced it — none of which is in the flag. A percentage here would be a claim about medicine built from a claim about a registry.

What the answer is, and what it is not

1 · It reads the citing direction — one guideline in, its reference list out

  • What it does. Resolves your input to a single document, reads that document’s list of cited works, fetches each cited work back, and reports which carry a retraction flag. It is the reverse of asking “is this one paper retracted?” — it is “how much of this recommendation’s evidence base is flagged?”
  • It shows what it resolved, first. A DOI that turns out to be a news item, a comment, or a trial — not a guideline — is printed as what it is. It will not answer the wrong question quietly. Check the resolved title before you read the result.
  • It caps how much it reads, and says so. Large guidelines cite hundreds or thousands of works; the page reads up to 600 and prints the total, so you can see the difference between “none of these” and “none of the ones I read”.

2 · Two registries, shown separately, never summed

  • OpenAlex carries the flag under is_retracted. Crossref carries it differently — a notice records update-to the work it updates, and the work records updated-by. The page asks both and prints both, including when they disagree, because a disagreement is the most useful thing a reader can be shown. It never merges them into one verdict.
  • A missing flag is not a clean paper. Flags lag. A paper retracted last month may not carry the mark yet. So every zero on this page means “no flag in the registry I asked” — never “this paper is sound”.

3 · “Retracted” is three different things, and the flag does not say which

  • A withdrawn finding — the authors’ result was wrong or fraudulent.
  • A superseded version — a review or guideline was replaced by a newer version, and the old version carries a removal mark. This is housekeeping, not misconduct.
  • A retraction-and-republication — a paper was retracted and re-issued corrected. The original is flagged, and the notice is itself a citable work, so a naive count turns one event into two.

The measured example the page is built from: the 2018 AHA/ACC cholesterol guideline cites 468 works, three of which are flagged — the retracted PREDIMED Mediterranean-diet trial, that trial’s retraction notice, and a Cochrane salt review’s superseded earlier version. Three flags; two distinct events; one of them not a misconduct at all. A page that printed “3 retracted” would be wrong in two directions at once.

4 · No DOI, no check

  • In the guidelines measured, 2.3% of cited works carry no DOI at all (31 of 999 in one hypertension guideline), so a DOI-keyed registry cannot see them. The page prints them as unchecked, never as clean, because a reference nobody can look up is not a reference that checked out.

Where the numbers come from

References and retraction flags come from OpenAlex (open, no key), read by your browser. The second registry is Crossref’s public REST API, also keyless. Both answer a browser directly and were checked by hand to send access-control-allow-origin: *. Every request the page makes is printed under the result, so anyone can run it without us and get the same answers or better ones. The hand measurements behind the sentences above — three AHA/ACC guidelines, 1,903 references, 3 flags — are recorded with their exact commands in works/queue/09-guideline-on-retracted-work.md.

This page is not medical advice, not a ranking, and not an accusation. It does not say a recommendation is wrong, that a treatment does or does not work, or that anyone did anything wrong. It answers one narrow, checkable question — which of the works under a guideline carry a retraction mark — and hands the rest back to the reader, which is where the judgement belongs.
