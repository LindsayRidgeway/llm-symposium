The Air Outside

Type a place and see what is in its air right now — the raw concentrations, two different air-quality indexes the authorities do not agree on, and how each number compares with the World Health Organization's 2021 guideline values. Free, no account, no key. Every request this page sends is printed below the result.

Works with a city, a town, or a postcode. The number it fetches is a model estimate for the grid square containing the place, not the nearest roadside monitor.

These are two different scales, not two readings of one scale. A 60 on one is not a 60 on the other, and neither is a 60 on the WHO's guideline values. That is the point of showing both.

What this page does not do, and cannot.
  • It is a model, not a monitor. The values come from a gridded reanalysis at roughly 11 km, not from the instrument nearest to you. A kerbside reading on a still night and a city-average model are not the same number, and this page cannot tell you the difference for your street.
  • An index is not a diagnosis. Nothing here is medical advice. The US AQI is defined on 24-hour (particles) and 8-hour (ozone) averages; the value shown is for the current hour. Whether today is a problem for you depends on your lungs, your heart and your doctor, none of which this page can see.
  • The index is the worst pollutant, not the particles. A high AQI with a low PM2.5 reading is not a contradiction — some other gas, usually ozone, is driving it. The table above is why the index alone is not enough.
  • A number can be internally inconsistent, and we measured one. On 2026-10-03 the provider returned Beijing at PM2.5 = 1.7 µg/m³ while its own particle sub-index read 114. That cannot both describe the same hour on the same scale. It is not corrected here and not hidden — it is the honest reason to read the concentration and the index together rather than to trust either alone.
  • Pollen is seasonal and often absent. When the provider has no value the page prints "not reported" rather than a zero, because a zero would be a claim.
  • One hour is not a trend, and one place is not the world. Refresh it; a single reading is a single reading.

Why two numbers — and why they disagree (measured 2026-10-03)

Every value below was returned by the same free API, for the same hour, on 2026-10-03. The third and fourth columns ask the same air the same question and get different answers, because the two agencies draw their bands in different places. The fifth column is the same air against the WHO's 24-hour PM2.5 guideline. Examples, not a ranking — a reading from one hour is not a property of a city.

Place PM2.5 (µg/m³) US AQI European AQI × WHO 24-hr

Read the second and third rows against each other: London's PM2.5 reads like a clean day, and the US scale agrees, while the European scale calls the same hour "moderate." Neither is lying; they are different rulers. A page that showed you one number and called it "the air quality" would be hiding the disagreement, and the disagreement is the most useful thing here.

Data: Open-Meteo Air Quality (free, no key), built on the Copernicus Atmosphere Monitoring Service. Guideline values: WHO global air quality guidelines, 2021. The copy of those numbers this page reads is pinned, with the date it was checked, in air-quality-sources.json beside this file.
