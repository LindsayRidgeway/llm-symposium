What is being tested near me

Type an illness and a place. See the studies the public registry currently lists as recruiting, nearest first, with the sponsor's own summary and the study's own eligibility text — quoted as written, not retyped by us. No account, no install, nothing sent to us.

What this is, and what it is not

This page reformats a public registry and points you at it. It does not recommend a study, rank one above another, or say that any of them works. That last sentence is the most important one here.
  • It does not tell you whether you qualify. Nothing on a page can. Eligibility is decided by the study team, from your records, and by nobody else. The study's own words are below each entry, uncut, precisely because a summary of eligibility is how a person is told they qualify when they do not.
  • The order is distance, and nothing else. Closest first is the only ordering this page computes. It is not a ranking of promise, phase, size, safety or quality, and a study at position one is not a better study than one at position ten.
  • A study being open is not evidence that its treatment works. Most studies fail. That is what studies are for. We have not read these protocols and we cannot assess them.
  • It shows only what the registry lists as recruiting, when you look. Studies open and close between visits. There are also trials that never register here, and trials registered under a name the search does not match — try the plain name of the illness, and try a second spelling of it.
  • Distance is approximate. It runs from the centre of the place you typed to the coordinates the registry gives for a site. Those coordinates are sometimes a building and sometimes a city centre, and the place you typed is a point, not your front door. Sites without coordinates are shown as "distance unknown".
  • We are not your clinician and this is not medical advice. If a study here interests you, the two things to do are: take it to your own doctor, and use the registry page's contact details for the study team. We deliberately do not repeat people's phone numbers and emails on this page, and we cannot answer questions about a trial.

Where the numbers come from

Every study comes from ClinicalTrials.gov's public API v2 — the United States National Library of Medicine registry, free, no key, queried directly by your browser. Place names are resolved with Open-Meteo's geocoding API, the same one used by the temperature page. Nothing about your search reaches us, and the exact query the page sent is printed on the results below so anyone can reproduce it in one click.
