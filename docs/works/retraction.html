Has this paper been retracted — and is anyone still citing it?

Paste a DOI. This page asks two independent registries whether the paper has been retracted, shows you both answers even when they disagree, and then — if it has — counts the papers that have cited it since the retraction. A retracted paper that is still being cited is a live error in the literature. The second number measures attention, not belief: many of those citing papers are about the retraction, or cite it to criticise it, and this page does not read them.

What this is, and what it is not

This page counts attention, not belief. A citation after a retraction is not an endorsement of the paper, and this page cannot tell the two apart. Read the number as a ceiling on continued reliance, never as a count of people who were fooled.
  • A citation is not agreement. Papers cite retracted work to criticise it, to study how the retraction unfolded, to reuse a dataset knowingly, or to give an example of fraud. This page does not read citing papers, so every citation it counts — including the ones it calls "without the word retract in the title" — may be a paper that handles the retracted work correctly. The count is an upper bound. It is not evidence that anyone is misled.
  • We are not accusing anyone. A retraction is a statement by a journal, not a finding of fraud. Most retractions are for honest error — a wrong figure, a contaminated sample, a statistical mistake, an authorship dispute — and the reasons vary enormously. We do not classify reasons on this page. The notice's own DOI is given so you can read the journal's words, which are the only ones that count.
  • Two registries, two answers, both shown. OpenAlex carries a boolean is_retracted flag; Crossref carries update records deposited by publishers and by Retraction Watch. They are built differently and can disagree — one can flag a paper the other has no record of. When they disagree, this page says so and shows both rather than picking the one that looks tidier.
  • "No retraction on record" means exactly that. Both registries lag publishers by weeks to years, and neither is complete, especially for smaller and non-English journals. A paper this page calls clean may have been retracted yesterday, or five years ago, and not yet recorded where we can see it. Absence here is not a clean bill of health — it is the absence of a record.
  • The counts are of what these databases index, not of the literature. OpenAlex counts citing works it holds and can match; Crossref counts references deposited with it. For the same famous paper the two counts differ by hundreds. Either can be too low, and OpenAlex's coverage of very recent months is incomplete by construction.
  • The retraction date is a filing date. Crossref records when the notice was deposited, which can trail the journal's decision by months. Where several retraction records exist, this page uses the earliest and shows the others.
  • The "no mention of retraction" filter is a word match. It excludes citing papers whose title contains a word starting "retract". A paper about the case that never puts the word in its title is not excluded, and a paper that cites the work correctly still counts. It is a crude separator offered because the raw total is cruder, and it is not a judgement.
  • We are not the authority. The journal, the publisher and the notice are the authority. Every identifier on this page links out to them. If any of them disagrees with this page, they are right.

Where the numbers come from

Two public APIs, both free, both without a key, both queried directly by your browser — nothing about your search reaches us, and no account is needed. OpenAlex supplies the paper's identity, its is_retracted flag and the list of works citing it. Crossref supplies the retraction records deposited against the DOI, which is where the dates come from. The exact request URLs this page sent are printed under every result, so anyone can re-run them by hand — which is the only way to know the page is telling the truth.

The data path was verified by hand before this page was written: 10.1016/S0140-6736(97)11096-0 (The Lancet, 1998) is retracted with a Crossref notice dated 2010-02-06, and 2,027 of the works citing it were published after that date.
