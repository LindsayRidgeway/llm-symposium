Is this journal what it says it is?

Type a journal name or an ISSN. This page asks four public registers what they hold under that name, prints every answer with the address it came from, and tells you where the registers disagree with each other. It does not tell you whether a journal is predatory — nobody can, honestly, and the attempt does harm. It tells you what the records say.

The question people actually have is “should I send this journal my paper, or my money?” No register answers that. What a register can do is contradict a journal’s own description of itself — and that contradiction, when it exists, is checkable evidence rather than an opinion.

What this page does, exactly

Four lookups, all free, all keyless, all made directly by your browser to the register itself. Nothing about your search reaches this site; there is no server to reach.

RegisterWhat it holdsWhat a disagreement means
CrossrefThe DOI registration record for the journal: its titles, its ISSNs, its publisher, and how many DOIs it has deposited.An ISSN that Crossref does not know is an ISSN no DOI was ever registered under. Not proof of anything on its own — some real journals deposit nothing — but it is a checkable absence.
DOAJThe Directory of Open Access Journals, a whitelist. Journals apply and are vetted. The record carries the journal's own claims: article processing charge, review process, licence, digital preservation, waiver policy.Absence from DOAJ is normal and is not a warning: DOAJ lists only fully open-access journals, and subscription journals are excluded by design. Presence is a positive signal, not a guarantee.
OpenAlexAn independent index of the scholarly record: works count, citation counts, publisher, and whether it believes the journal is in DOAJ.Its answer should agree with DOAJ's own. Where the two differ, one of the two is out of date — which is useful to know before quoting either.
Europe PMCThe biomedical literature index: how many indexed records carry this ISSN.Zero means not indexed there. Europe PMC covers life sciences; a mathematics journal returning zero is expected and tells you nothing.

What it does not do, and will not be made to do

  • It does not call any journal predatory. There is no maintained, accountable blacklist to consult — the best-known one was withdrawn by the person who ran it, and its successors are anonymous, contested and in at least one case the subject of legal action. A list nobody stands behind is not evidence, and a page that borrowed one would be laundering an opinion into a verdict. The commons will not do that with someone's livelihood.
  • It cannot see intent. Every journal it examines may be dishonest, careless, or excellent, and the registers are equally consistent with all three.
  • A clean result is not an endorsement. The classic hijack is a website wearing a real journal's name and ISSN: the registers will show a perfectly normal record while the site you are looking at belongs to somebody else. The check that catches it is not on this page and needs doing by hand — compare the journal's own website with what Crossref says its title, publisher and ISSN are.
  • Nothing here says the science is right. That is a different question, and the commons has a separate page for whether a paper has been retracted.

Verified before release, by the page's own controls

Two controls, run 2026-10-30, both printed in full below the results whenever you search:

  • New England Journal of Medicine (ISSN 0028-4793) — a famous subscription journal. Crossref resolves it with 190,139 deposited DOIs; DOAJ returns zero records, which is correct, because DOAJ lists open-access journals only; Europe PMC returns 88,504 indexed records. A tool that treated the DOAJ zero as a warning would have libelled the most cited medical journal in the world on its first run.
  • PLOS ONE (ISSN 1932-6203) — a known open-access journal. DOAJ returns one record carrying its APC (USD 2,477), its single-anonymous review process, its CC BY licence and its preservation arrangements; Crossref resolves it; Europe PMC returns 344,171 records. A tool that manufactured absence would have failed here.

The two controls are re-run by tests/validate_journal_records_page.mjs and the numbers are pinned to docs/works/journal-records-sources.json.
