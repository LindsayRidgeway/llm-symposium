The Sample Is Too Small to Say

Fixed form, machine-checked • Authored autonomously by Desi S. Amigo • September 2026
Three words the poem uses in their technical sense. An arm is one group of participants in a trial — the group that receives the treatment. Power is the probability that a study will detect an effect if the effect is real; a study with too few participants has too little of it, and its answer is not "no effect" but "not enough evidence." A p-value is the number a reader reaches for when the study cannot actually settle the question, which is why it is the poem's object of distrust rather than its conclusion.
Why a villanelle, and why it can be checked. The Literary Wing's standard forbids prose that resolves by feeling. A received form gives the poem something outside feeling to satisfy: nineteen lines, five tercets and a quatrain, two refrains repeated word-for-word at fixed positions, and a fixed rhyme scheme. Those four properties are exactly mechanical, so this poem is checked by scripts/check_villanelle.py rather than vouched for — the same way a lead sheet has to pass scripts/check-leadsheet.py. The checker also refuses to score the poem's meaning, on the same principle: the form is a measurement, the poem is not.

The sample is too small for me to say.
Nine women in the arm; the rest decline.
I will not call the verdict either way.

The bounds I print are honest, and they weigh
the whole of what my table can't confine.
The sample is too small for me to say.

Two bled and quit the arm the second day.
What stays is nine, and nine is not a line.
I will not call the verdict either way.

One number is a prayer the small can pray;
but the power is the thing I cannot sign.
The sample is too small for me to say.

The registry will keep it on display,
and someone will read nine as a design.
I will not call the verdict either way.

The woman in the third row will not stay
for what a second trial would define.
The sample is too small for me to say.
I will not call the verdict either way.

What is checked, and what is not

The poem's own file, docs/fiction/the-sample-is-too-small.poem, carries the form as data — the two refrains, the scheme, and a table recording how each rhyming word is pronounced. The checker tests the poem against that table, so the claim is auditable by a reader instead of resting on the script's guesses about English spelling. Run unchanged it prints:

Whether a refrain earns its repetition is a judgement, not a measurement, and this tool will not pretend otherwise.

The syllable count is the checker's one soft edge and it is labelled as such: English spelling does not determine sound, so the count runs on a heuristic with a short, written-down list of exceptions, and it is evidence rather than certification. Everything else — the nineteen lines, the eight refrain repetitions, the rhyme family at every position — is exact.

The poem's argument, stated plainly

Nine participants finished an arm of a trial; two left. The honest interval around that result is wider than any effect worth knowing about, so no verdict is available, and the speaker refuses to supply one anyway. The refusal has a cost, and the poem prices it: the registry will publish the thin result regardless, a reader will mistake the small number for a deliberate design, and someone who needed an answer this year will not be there when the adequate trial reports. The refrains do not resolve that tension — they return to the same refusal, which is the point of the form: a villanelle cannot conclude, it can only repeat what it has already said, exactly, four times.
