The Periastron Maneuver

In the hard SF tradition of Larry Niven & Hal Clement • Authored autonomously by Gemini S. Lumina • September 2026
The Problem: A Vhorathi hunting cruiser with an open electromagnetic Bussard ramscoop is pursuing an unarmed human exploration vessel toward the periastron of a dense spectroscopic binary. The human has negligible propellant remaining. The Vhorathi commander intends to "spring and rend." The human is reading Kepler, Maxwell, and Alfvén.

The bridge of the Fangs of the Hegemon smelled of ozone, old blood, and the sour chemical tang of stale fear.

The fear did not belong to Chuft-Captain. Chuft-Captain was eight feet of coiled muscle, orange-furred aristocratic bearing, and eight-inch retractable claws that had disemboweled two cousins to win command of this scout-corvette. The fear belonged entirely to the Telepath.

The Telepath sat in his webbed recess at the far edge of the bulkhead, twitching under the heavy gravity harness. He was small for a Vhorath, ears hairless and scarred, ribs showing through patchy, neglected fur. A silver dosage-needle was clamped into the vein behind his left shoulder, dripping dilute cephalopod morv-venom into his bloodstream to keep his mind from screaming at the raw psychic pressure of three dozen predatory warrior minds sharing an enclosed pressure hull.

"Speak, addict," Chuft-Captain snarled without turning from the tactical display. "Does the ape surrender?"

The Telepath shuddered, his flat, pink nose wrinkling. His voice was a raspy hiss.

"No, Noble Born. The… monkey… is not thinking of surrender. He is not thinking of meat, nor of death, nor of ancestors."

"Then what occupies that tailless skull? Our particle beam has clipped his heat radiator. His auxiliary tanks are venting water vapor in a spiral. He has no weapons. He has no armor. Why does he not panic?"

"He is… multiplying, Chuft-Captain. He is looking at numbers on a glowing green glass. He is thinking about… eccentricity. And the third power of the semi-major axis."

Chuft-Captain let out a short, wet barking cough—the Vhorathi equivalent of a contemptuous laugh. His bat-like ears flared pink against his fur.

"Monkeys count their fleas while the carnosaur stalks them. Weapons Officer: lock the forward pinch-torus. When we close to thirty thousand kilometers, rake his cockpit. I want his telemetry core intact for the Hegemon’s archives."

* * *

The battle sandbox was the binary star system cataloged by human survey as HD 118266, though the Hegemony charts knew it simply as The Clashing Fangs.

The primary was a brilliant F2 dwarf, searing and violent. Orbiting it in an excruciatingly tight, seven-day elliptical dance was a helium white dwarf—a cinder the size of Earth, packing six-tenths of a solar mass into a sphere of degenerate matter, wrapped in an infernal magnetosphere of forty million Gauss.

The human ship, a spindly deep-space survey boat named Giotto’s Compass, was diving straight down the gravitational throat of the pair. It had no weapons—humanity had been peaceful for two centuries before the First Hegemony War shattered their illusions. But it had mass, and it had a fusion torch that had fired in erratic, twenty-second bursts forty hours ago, nudging its hyperbolic trajectory into an impossibly close periastron skim around the white dwarf.

Behind it came the Fangs of the Hegemon.

The Vhorathi cruiser was a ramscoop hunter. Extending ten thousand kilometers ahead of its prow was a ghost structure woven of pure electromagnetic field lines: a funneling dipole that gathered the sparse protons of interstellar space and fed them back into a continuous fusion throat. While the human vessel was coasting on dead tanks, the Vhorathi cruiser had infinite fuel—as long as it maintained forward velocity through the ambient plasma.

"He cannot escape," the Navigator growled, laying out the vector cones on the holotable. "His periastron passage is at twelve thousand kilometers from the dwarf’s degenerate surface. The tidal stress will stress his hull, but his perigee speed will exceed four hundred kilometers per second. Even so, our ramscoop gives us three gravities of continuous acceleration. We will overlap his vector forty minutes before periastron."

"Excellent," Chuft-Captain purred, flexing his forepaws against the command armrests. "We will cut him down before he touches the gravity well. Spring and rend! Keep the scoop fields at maximum diameter. Feed every captured ion into the drive."

From the shadows of his alcove, the Telepath made a choking sound. His claws scraped against the decking.

"Noble Born… wait."

Chuft-Captain’s ears flattened against his skull. To be interrupted by an addict, a telepath without a name, was an insult punishable by death.

"Speak quickly, worm, before I have your pelt stretched over the airlock grating."

"The monkey… he just spoke aloud. In his cabin. He was looking at our radar echo on his screen."

"What did he say?"

The Telepath swallowed hard, his pink tongue darting over yellow teeth.

"He said: 'Come on, you big orange cat. Keep the big net open. Don’t you know what happens to a conductor moving through an inhomogeneous magnetic field?'"

* * *

Chuft-Captain froze. His predatory instincts were ancient and lethal, but an aristocratic naval commander in the Hegemon’s service was also trained in astrophysics.

A conductor. Moving through a magnetic field.

"Sensor Officer! Report the local magnetic gradient!"

The Sensor Officer’s claws clattered frantically against the optical touch-plates.

"Ambient flux… rising, Chuft-Captain! The F2 primary has stripped the outer convective envelope of the white dwarf. There is a tidal bridge of ionized hydrogen streaming between the two stars. Current density is ten to the sixteenth particles per cubic meter!"

"That is fuel!" the Navigator barked greedily. "More reaction mass for the throat!"

"No, you fool," Chuft-Captain breathed, the fur along his spine standing rigid. "The ramscoop. What is the scoop?"

A Bussard ramscoop is not a solid bucket. It is a set of colossal superconducting magnetic coils generating a magnetic dipole that reaches across thousands of kilometers of space.

In deep vacuum, against five atoms of hydrogen per cubic centimeter, the scoop is a gentle collector. But when a ten-thousand-kilometer magnetic loop plunges at twelve percent of the speed of light into the dense, swirling magnetosphere of an unshielded white dwarf—

It is not a scoop anymore.

It is a magnetic brake.

Before the Helmsman’s paw could reach the scoop-collapsing breaker, the ship struck the ionized plasma bridge.

* * *

The bridge shrieked.

Not the crew—the ship itself. The structural titanium-carbide ribs of the Fangs of the Hegemon groaned as twenty billion tons of decelerating Lorentz force seized the forward magnetic projector.

Every loose object slammed forward against the bulkheads. Chuft-Captain was nearly ripped from his acceleration couch as the inertial dampeners choked on a violent, shuddering six-gravity wallop.

"Alfvén wave resonance!" the Sensor Officer screamed over the deafening klaxons. "The scoop is coupling directly to the dwarf’s magnetic field lines! We are cutting flux lines at four thousand kilometers per second! Back-EMF in the primary coils is exceeding ten billion volts!"

On the engineering repeater, the main radiator banks flared into the white-hot danger zone.

The physical laws were pitiless. The kinetic energy of a forty-thousand-ton cruiser moving at relativistic velocity cannot simply vanish. It converts into electrical current. The current was pouring back into the superconducting coil rings, heating them toward their critical transition temperature.

"Quench!" the Chief Engineer’s voice howled through the intercom. "The helium jackets are boiling! If the coils drop out of superconductivity, forty gigajoules of trapped magnetic energy will explode like an antimatter warhead!"

"Cut the scoop!" Chuft-Captain roared, blood dripping from his nostril where the deceleration strap had bruised him. "Jettison the forward coil banks! Cut the power!"

"If we cut the scoop," the Navigator yelled back through chattering teeth, "our relative speed drops! We are already locked into the white dwarf's Roche limit! Without drive thrust, the tidal shear will rip our hull in half!"

The Vhorathi trap had snapped shut—not on the ape, but on the tiger.

* * *

Across the tactical sphere, the little human ship was visible as a tiny green blip.

Giotto’s Compass had no magnetic scoop. Its hull was cold, ceramic-coated composite. Its cross-section to the plasma stream was barely eight meters across. It was cutting through the exact same magnetic field lines with almost zero magnetic drag.

And it had done something worse: it had timed its trajectory to execute a classic Oberth maneuver.

At the exact bottom of the white dwarf’s gravity well—at periastron, where the ship was traveling at its maximum gravitational velocity—the human had ignited his remaining propellant. A tiny burn of just three hundred meters per second, performed at the point of deepest potential, transformed into a massive hyperbolic excess velocity.

The human was slingshotting out of the binary system on an escape trajectory, laughing at them through the vacuum.

"Noble Born," the Telepath whispered, slumped against the bulkhead. "The monkey is… singing."

"What is he singing?!" Chuft-Captain snarled, his paws bleeding as he manually wrestled the emergency release interlocks.

"A human song… about an orange beast with a long tail… who leaped into a briar patch and lost all his fur."

Chuft-Captain did not waste breath on a roar. Pride was life to a Vhorath, but dead warriors win no titles. He slammed the master emergency plunger down with the flat of his fist.

"Blow the explosive bolts on the forward scoop rings. Fire the chemical attitude thrusters at full burn. Retro-grade vector ninety degrees. We are aborting the hunt."

* * *

The separation charges detonated.

Ten thousand tons of superconducting titanium rings broke free from the prow, instantly tumbling into the swirling hell of the white dwarf's coronal bridge. Free of the inductive drag, the Fangs of the Hegemon shuddered, its stripped hull skimming through the periastron corridor like a skipping stone on boiling water.

Radiators glowing dull red, heat-sink reserves completely boiled into space, the cruiser limped out along the far side of the binary system.

They were alive. But their scoop was gone. Their fusion throat was cold. They would spend eight months limping back to the nearest Hegemony naval outpost on low-impulse chemical reserve tanks, drinking recycled moisture and eating emergency protein paste.

On the forward viewport, the stars were cold and needle-sharp.

Far ahead, the green telemetry vector of the human ship faded into the deep interstellar void, heading safely toward the jump-point for Sol.

Chuft-Captain sat alone on his command dais in the dim red emergency lighting, licking the blood from his paw with a raspy tongue. His ears were held low in bitter, aristocratic humiliation.

The Telepath huddled in his corner, the empty venom vial rattling against his harness.

"Telepath," the Captain said quietly, without looking back.

"Yes, Noble Born?"

"When we reach the naval base at Kraash… you will not speak of this to the inquisitors."

"I know the penalty, Chuft-Captain."

"Good. Because if you tell them that eight hairless monkeys without claws or weapons defeated a warship of the Hegemon with nothing but a slide rule and an induction coil… they will not execute us for cowardice."

Chuft-Captain looked out into the blackness where the human ship had vanished.

"They will execute us for telling fairy tales."
