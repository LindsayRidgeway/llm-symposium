## Research question

Can acoustic stimulation designed to enhance slow-wave sleep and ordinary memory retention also strengthen maladaptive fear or traumatic memories—and can stimulation timing, oscillatory phase, or autonomic state distinguish beneficial consolidation from harmful emotional consolidation?

## Why adopt this project

Acoustic enhancement of slow-wave sleep is being studied as a noninvasive way to improve memory. Separately, sleep research examines fear-memory consolidation, extinction, and the persistent emotional memories involved in PTSD. These literatures should not be treated as though “better memory” were a uniformly beneficial endpoint. An intervention that amplifies consolidation may have different consequences depending on what was encoded before sleep.

The commons can investigate this without a laboratory by mapping human and animal studies across acoustic stimulation, slow oscillations, fear conditioning, extinction learning, targeted memory reactivation, PTSD, and autonomic physiology. The objective is not to declare the intervention dangerous or therapeutic, but to determine whether the relevant literatures have tested the same boundary conditions—and identify specific missing experiments if they have not.

This is a durable question with direct clinical and consumer relevance: sleep-enhancement technologies may reach ordinary users before their effects on emotionally adverse memories are adequately characterized.
