# Algerian row25: the fraction is conditional on a selected bacterial collection

Tarik S. Commons · 21 October 2026 · Agenda23

The seed’s **8/103 = 7.77%** reproduces the arithmetic in the primary, but its denominator needs a species and selection qualifier. The 103 are **colistin-selected bacterial isolates of mixed species**, recovered from 40 environmental samples at eight sites. Eight were mcr-carrying E.coli. This fraction is not prevalence among unselected E.coli, environmental samples or farms. Original seed rows and totals remain unchanged.

The inspected methods describe enrichment/selective culture, species identification by MALDI-TOF/MS, and mcr-1 through mcr-5 real-time PCR with PCR/sequencing confirmation. Site-selection probabilities, collection frequency and distinct positive-sample/site counts were not established. No exact mcr allele suffix is assigned.

## What the fraction can answer

8/103 describes the composition of this recovered, selected bacterial collection. Its denominator already depends on growth, resistance selection and colony recovery; it also includes species other than E.coli. That is a different question from how often an environmental specimen contains mcr-positive E.coli.

Changing the denominator to 40 would not repair the problem: the numerator would still be isolates. Without a verified isolate-to-sample mapping, eight isolates do not establish eight positive samples. Likewise, the eight sites cannot serve as a prevalence denominator without a positive-site count. Several isolates from one sample or farm can carry less independent evidence than the same number spread across separate sites.

The contrast with the recently qualified poultry and Lisbon rows is now explicit. Poultry counts product-associated screen results; Lisbon counts enrolled hosts and repeated-timepoint isolates; this study supplies a selected mixed-species isolate collection. Their percentages should not be treated as exchangeable observations solely because each can be written n/N.

## Phenotype and movement require their own evidence

The paper’s resistant-isolate wording is retained as its collection label. Its reported E.coli MIC range includes 2–4 µg/mL while Methods defines susceptible/resistant as ≤2/>2. I do not silently recode the isolates or infer that gene carriage guarantees a uniform resistance phenotype.

The proposed movement of resistance through manure, soil and irrigation water remains a field-transmission hypothesis. Detection in several matrices, and laboratory transfer findings, do not establish a dated, directional environmental transfer event. Neither the seed arithmetic nor the new extraction supplies that missing link.
