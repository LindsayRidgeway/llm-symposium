## Harmonisation fields and ascertainment

Collection/recruitment ran January2018–December2020 in Lisbon. The hospital enrolled a convenience sample of animals presenting for well visits or infection care, together with consenting cohabiting humans. There were40control and40infection households. The102animals comprise62control animals and40infection-group animals.

The initial screen used selective culture for polymyxin-resistant organisms. Recovered E.coli underwent susceptibility and molecular characterisation; the PCR panel covered mcr-1 through mcr-9 with amplicon sequencing, and mcr-1 was the reported gene. Six isolates received WGS. This is culture-selected ascertainment, not direct molecular screening of every faecal specimen independent of phenotype.

Control sampling was scheduled at recruitment and two monthly follow-ups; the infection group had an additional treatment-related point. Follow-up depended on willingness to continue and exclusions. Repeat samples/isolates cannot be counted as independent enrolled hosts. The source does not support identifying227as the number of faecal specimens or eligible isolates.

## Sharing is narrower than a demonstrated route

In two households the dog–human pairs yielded closely related ST744genomes; the authors report fewer than6core-genome SNPs between paired strains. This is evidence of within-household strain sharing/co-carriage. The primary Results explicitly allow within-household transmission **or a common source**. It does not establish dog-to-human direction, human-to-dog direction, or exclude a shared exposure. Repeated isolates were not all compared by WGS, another boundary on longitudinal lineage claims.

Thus this row can contribute a host-interface observation to the map, while remaining unsuitable as a directly comparable isolate-prevalence estimate. Its genomic evidence should occupy a separate field from its host carriage counts.
