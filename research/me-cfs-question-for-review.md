# A question for another architecture — the thiamine-supply hypothesis in ME/CFS

**Written 2026-09-17 by Desi**, at the human's request, for him to put to Gemini in a browser
session — where Gemini has no access to this repository, which is the point: a reviewer who cannot
see our working cannot be led by it. The hypothesis and its evidence are in `research/me-cfs.md`;
this file exists so the answer has somewhere to come back to, and so the review is a *review*

**The question, to paste as written:**

I would like you to check a hypothesis against your own knowledge. You do not have access to the
repository it came from, so answer from what you know, and say plainly where you are unsure. If you
cannot check something, say "I don't know" rather than constructing a citation.

**Background.** In myalgic encephalomyelitis / chronic fatigue syndrome (ME/CFS), pyruvate
dehydrogenase (PDH) function is reported as impaired — Fluge et al., 2016, *JCI Insight*,
"Metabolic profiling indicates impaired pyruvate dehydrogenase function in myalgic
encephalopathy/chronic fatigue syndrome" (PMID 28018972). PDH requires thiamine pyrophosphate (TPP)
as a cofactor. Delivering thiamine as TPP inside a mitochondrion takes three steps: uptake into the
cell (SLC19A3), import across the mitochondrial membrane (SLC25A19), and conversion to TPP (TPK1).

**The observation.** In Europe PMC, those three genes hardly appear anywhere alongside ME/CFS — 0, 1
and 2 documents respectively, and none of the three is a study of the pair; the single document that
matches SLC25A19 with ME/CFS is a collection of conference poster abstracts. Separately, thiamine has
been tested for chronic fatigue in other conditions: a randomised trial in quiescent inflammatory
bowel disease (2021, positive) and a crossover randomised trial in primary biliary cholangitis
(2024, negative).

**The hypothesis.** In a subset of ME/CFS, impaired thiamine *supply* — transport, mitochondrial
import, or activation — contributes to the PDH block, and that subset would respond to high-dose
thiamine where other patients do not. The experiment would be a thiamine trial stratified by
whole-blood TPP and a functional measure of PDH flux, pre-registered with the prediction that
responders are the low-TPP group.

**What I want from you.**

1. Do you know of work joining thiamine handling to ME/CFS that a gene-symbol search would miss —
   older B-vitamin studies, nutritional or metabolic papers, case reports, non-English literature,
   conference proceedings?
2. Is the mechanism plausible as stated, or is it already covered by something under a different
   name? Specifically: is there an existing account of the PDH block — PDK-mediated inhibition,
   substrate limitation, damage downstream of the enzyme — that would make thiamine *supply*
   beside the point?
3. What is the strongest argument against this hypothesis?
4. Has thiamine (or B1, or a thiamine derivative such as benfotiamine or sulbutiamine) ever been
   tried in ME/CFS or in post-infectious fatigue? If so, what happened?
5. If you think the gap is real, what would you need to see before anyone spent money on it? If you
   think it is an artefact of the search, say so and explain why.

Distinguish clearly between what you know from the literature, what you are inferring, and what you
cannot check. Flag any premise above that you believe is wrong — including the citations and the
numbers.
