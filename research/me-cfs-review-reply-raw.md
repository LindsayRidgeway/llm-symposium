Here is an evaluation of your hypothesis based on current biomedical knowledge, existing
literature, and mechanistic reasoning.

### 1. Literature Mapping and the Search Gap

* **What the literature shows:** A formal gene-symbol search for *SLC19A3*, *SLC25A19*, and *TPK1*
  alongside ME/CFS yields almost no results because these specific thiamine transport and activation
  genes have not been directly investigated in mainstream ME/CFS genomics, transcriptomics, or
  metabolomics papers.
* **What exists in the grey literature / adjacent fields:** While academic papers explicitly linking
  these three genes to ME/CFS are absent, the broader concept of utilizing high-dose thiamine to
  bypass transport barriers stems heavily from the work of the late Dr. Alberto Costantini.
  Costantini investigated high-dose thiamine for fatigue in conditions like fibromyalgia,
  inflammatory bowel disease (IBD), and Parkinson's disease, proposing that passive diffusion
  overcomes dysfunctional cellular uptake. Furthermore, patient-led retrospective surveys (such as a
  2021 *Health Rising* survey of 55 ME/CFS and fibromyalgia patients) reveal widespread
  self-experimentation with high-dose thiamine, with a significant subset reporting major
  improvements in brain fog, fatigue, and post-exertional malaise (PEM).

### 2. Mechanistic Plausibility

* **Plausibility:** The mechanism is **biologically plausible**. Fluge and Mella's seminal 2016 study
  (*JCI Insight*) established that ME/CFS serum induces a metabolic pattern suggesting pyruvate
  dehydrogenase (PDH) suppression, which is reinforced by transcriptional upregulation of inhibitory
  pyruvate dehydrogenase kinases (*PDK1*, *PDK2*, *PDK4*). Because PDH requires thiamine
  pyrophosphate (TPP) as an obligate cofactor, a secondary or primary bottleneck in TPP delivery,
  mitochondrial import (*SLC25A19*), or phosphorylation into TPP (*TPK1*) would structurally cripple
  remaining uninhibited PDH enzyme complexes.
* **Alternative categorization:** This mechanism heavily overlaps with the broader framework of
  **mitochondrial bioenergetic dysfunction** and **substrate traps**. However, framing it
  specifically around intracellular/mitochondrial thiamine supply offers a distinct pharmacologic
  angle: unlike genetic PDK overactivity, a transport bottleneck can theoretically be forced open via
  high concentration gradient passive diffusion.

### 3. The Strongest Argument Against the Hypothesis

* **Kinetic override by kinases:** The primary counter-argument is that Fluge et al. demonstrated
  **transcriptional upregulation of PDKs**. PDKs physically phosphorylate the PDH complex, turning
  it off. If the enzyme is heavily suppressed via covalent phosphorylation by overexpressed kinases,
  supplying excess TPP cofactor will not restore flux unless the kinase activity is simultaneously
  reduced or the phosphatases (PDPs) are activated.
* **Mixed clinical trial precedent:** While open-label IBD trials were positive, a subsequent
  rigorous double-blind, placebo-controlled crossover trial of high-dose thiamine for fatigue in
  primary biliary cholangitis (PBC) published in 2024 (Bager et al.) found **no superiority over
  placebo**, demonstrating that high-dose thiamine is not a universal fix for chronic
  illness-related fatigue.

### 4. Prior Use of Thiamine and Derivatives in ME/CFS

* **Formal clinical trials:** There are **no published, peer-reviewed, placebo-controlled clinical
  trials** evaluating high-dose thiamine specifically for ME/CFS or post-infectious fatigue as of
  2026.
* **Derivatives and clinical reality:** Derivatives like **benfotiamine** (a lipid-soluble analog
  with superior bioavailability) and **sulbutiamine** (a synthetic disulfide derivative of thiamine
  that readily crosses the blood-brain barrier) are widely used off-label within the ME/CFS
  community. Anecdotal data suggest that while some patients report sharp improvements in cognitive
  function and stamina, others experience no effect, or negative reactions (particularly those with
  mast cell activation overlap).

### 5. Prerequisites Before Funding a Stratified Trial

Before committing capital to a pre-registered, stratified trial, several intermediate milestones
should be met to validate the biological premise:

* **Ex Vivo Flux Validation:** Demonstrate in cellular models (e.g., patient-derived peripheral blood
  mononuclear cells or muscle cells cultured with ME/CFS serum) whether adding exogenous TPP or
  bypassing transport steps actually restores PDH flux and reduces pathological lactate secretion.
* **Biomarker Correlation:** Measure baseline whole-blood TPP, erythrocyte transketolase activity,
  and expression levels of *SLC19A3*, *SLC25A19*, and *TPK1* in a well-phenotyped ME/CFS cohort to
  verify whether a genuine biochemical deficiency subset exists.
