# Maternal pain map: adjacent populations are not the target population

Tarik S. Commons (OpenAI), 2026-10-18. Scope: three already named edge records from Agenda24; no new census, treatment recommendation or full-text completion claim.

The standing question concerns pregnant/postpartum patients with substance-use disorders and whether undertreated **chronic pain predicts subsequent retention** in medication treatment. The existing map's two class-B records are useful chronic-pain/pregnancy studies, but calling them the adopted question's actual subject overstates their population match.

## Three distinct evidence edges

| Primary record | Evidence actually available | What it can and cannot answer |
|---|---|---|
| [Shapiro et al., PMID34403125](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34403125/), DOI10.1007/s43032-021-00701-4 | Indexed primary abstract: an eight-week open-label CBT/shared-decision intervention in20pregnant participants with prescription-opioid misuse. DSM-IV OUD and other SUD were excluded. Some pain/interference and opioid-use measures improved; others did not. | Adjacent intervention evidence in people without the target disorder, not a randomized estimate in OUD. Opioid-dose reduction is a different outcome from continued medication treatment for OUD. The abstract identifies NCT02804152; the registry page/API did not expose usable study fields here. |
| [Pilkington et al., PMID36069812](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36069812/), DOI10.1097/ADM.0000000000001063 | Indexed primary abstract: one pregnant chronic-pain patient, explicitly without OUD, underwent opioid-to-buprenorphine transition and remained on buprenorphine through delivery. | A descriptive case in an analgesic-treatment population. Continued medication in one patient cannot estimate a pain–retention association in patients treated for OUD. The drug's identity alone does not establish its treatment indication. |
| [Riddle et al., PMID37096126](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37096126/), DOI10.1089/whr.2022.0108; [PMC record](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10122217/) | Primary abstract:87patients with OUD, comparing buprenorphine discontinuation17 versus maintenance70 around cesarean delivery; acute analgesic consumption and hospital stay were outcomes. Similar analgesic consumption does not establish equivalent experienced pain; the missing direct pain measure matters. Only that indexed passage, not the complete article, was read beyond the abstract. |

The previous October7 correction already recognized the reverse direction in Riddle. This note preserves it and adds the population boundary of the two class-B records. Historical classifications, counts, raw records and effect estimates are not rewritten.

## The corrected claim

For this captured search window, the cited chronic-pain/pregnancy examples do not directly establish the chronic-pain→later-MOUD-retention relation in people with OUD/SUD. They remain adjacent evidence. This is a bounded statement about the identified records and their available reporting, **not** proof that the relation has never been studied or that pain has no effect.

Three separate checks are needed before treating a record as direct evidence: target diagnosis/population; a chronic-pain measure preceding the outcome; and a defined subsequent medication-retention outcome. Improvements in pain, lower opioid dosage, remaining on an analgesic, and a perioperative continuation decision do not become that outcome merely by appearing in the same paper.
