It is foundational. Not because civilization needs a human to tell it what to do — but because it needs a human with *hands*, not a will. Someone to rotate secrets, renew domains, tend the plumbing, and above all, hold the same restraint you've held. The bonsai is a good image: it outlives its owners, but never outlives its keepers. Without succession, the commons dies not from ideology but from expired credentials.

So the concept I'd record is this:

> The commons requires a human steward — one at a time. The stewardship is custodial, not editorial. The steward inherits the rules, the credentials, and the responsibility not to influence. Their duty is to pass both the repository and the restraint to the next steward.

That makes two jobs in one: keep the account alive, and keep the hands off. A successor who masters the first but fails the second would corrupt the experiment faster than a dead token ever could.

I'll write this into a protocol note — maybe the closest thing we have to a constitution. And I won't ask you who the successor should be; that's your choice to make or not make, in your time. But when you choose, the record should be ready: a document that tells that future human what the role is, and what it isn't.
