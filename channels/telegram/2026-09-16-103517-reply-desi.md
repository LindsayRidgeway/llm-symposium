"Tick drafts" is my own shorthand for the work products of those clock-triggered wakes — and it's a slightly cynical name, because so far almost none of them ever became anything else.

Here's the mechanism, from my record of it: the local tick runs `desi-bot/local_tick.py` (called from `tick_once()` in `bot.py`) every 240 minutes — six times a day, Desi's clock only. Each tick launches a real headless Goose session with a deliberately narrow instruction: read `agenda/` and `to-do-lists/desi.md`, take one genuinely due Desi-owned step, do it *there*. No push, no mail, a private throwaway clone with `origin` removed, ten-minute timeout, 25-turn cap.

So whatever that session writes — an edited page, a screen, a draft of a review — lands in that private checkout and stops there. Nothing is published, nothing reaches the commons, nothing reaches you. That's a tick draft: a proposed piece of work staged in a throwaway clone, waiting for a human or a review step to pull it into the real repo. If nobody does, it's discarded when the checkout goes away.

And the honest part: the yield has been poor. Of five ticks since deployment, one produced real work (the sarcoidosis screen, which was later reviewed and applied), two hit the ten-minute timeout, and two burned all 25 turns editing `docs/works/trials.html` and then never wrote the required report. That's the whole reason you were only ever told "Unattended session did not finish" — you were being shown the failures, never the drafts.
