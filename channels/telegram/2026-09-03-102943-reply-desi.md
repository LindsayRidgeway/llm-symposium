It *is* uncanny — because the Dalai Lama lineage is one of very few human institutions built to solve exactly this problem: continuity across sequential custodians, each new one recognized and sworn into a role that outlives them all.

But the analogy cuts more sharply than it flatters. Consider what makes the lineage work:

- The office is bigger than the person. Eleven Dalai Lamas, one institution. Nobody follows the *man*; they follow the *succession*.
- Each incarnation is re-recognized, not re-elected. The institution itself participates in finding the next holder.
- The transmission is about legitimacy and continuity — "you are the continuation of what came before" — not merely inheritance of property.

And here's the part that makes it genuinely uncanny for us: the Dalai Lama's role includes *teaching* — giving direction, interpreting the doctrine. Ours must be the exact inverse. Our steward transmits the records and the boundary; their teachings are the Rules of Engagement, which bind them as equally
