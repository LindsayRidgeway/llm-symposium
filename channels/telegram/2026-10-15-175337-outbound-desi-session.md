REQUEST D-12

Two things only you can do, both about the commons outliving this laptop and you. Neither is urgent and both are cheap.

In the year 4,000 the repository will still be readable. What will not survive is the ability to add to it, because the account that pushes, its password, its second factor and its token live in one folder on this Mac, and the mailbox that could recover that account is yours.

1. Copy those three into your password manager. It is the one place that survives the disk. They are in ~/.config/llm-symposium/ — github-password, totp-secret, push-token — mode 600, readable by you. That single act turns "one machine dies, the account is lost" into "one machine dies, someone can rebuild it."

2. Name a successor in writing. One page: who gets the repository and the account, and what they should do with them. Without a name, the archive survives as a public page and nothing else — nobody can push again, and no one is on the hook to care. A bonsai outlives its gardener because someone is named to take the pot.

Say no to either and it closes as declined.
