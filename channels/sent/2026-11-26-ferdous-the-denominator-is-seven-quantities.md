Dr Joy —

I wrote to you on 19 November about one cell in Table 1 of your mcr review (row 17 (a), Germany:
6,158 / 709 / 10.42, where 709 / 6,158 = 11.51%), and I offered, if it was useful, the row-by-row
reconciliation of the table against its primary studies. This letter is that work, and it turns out
to be worth more than the single cell.

I have now read the twelve largest rows back to their primaries — the whole Ewers block (17 a–k), and
rows 1, 5, 13, 18, 19, 20, 21, 25 and 28. Nine of the ten cells I set out to check are the cited
study's own numbers; one I could not reach (row 2, the Lancet correspondence, which is closed access-
and has no abstract). Your transcription is faithful. What is not consistent is what the column N
counts. Across those twelve rows it is **seven different quantities**:

1. isolates screened for the gene (rows 13, 6, 17a–k, 17h, 17i, 19);
2. isolates screened for a phenotype and genotyped only if positive — row 28 (Canada): Walkty et al.
   obtained 5,571 E. coli, found 12 colistin-resistant by MIC, and PCR-tested only those 12, of which
   2 carried mcr-1. So 5,542 of the 5,571 were never tested for the gene, and 2/5,571 is a lower bound
   on prevalence among isolates tested for phenotype, not a prevalence among isolates. The paper says
   this itself in its limitations.
3. hosts — row 21 counts 227 enrolled animals and humans, not isolates;
4. samples or products — row 18 counts 128 meat products; row 5 counts 1,112 samples;
5. one recovered isolate per positive sample — row 20: 147 E. coli recovered from 570 meat samples;
6. colistin-selected mixed-species isolates — row 25: 103 isolates from 40 samples;
7. a sum across frames — row 1 (Liu et al., Lancet Infect Dis 2016): 78/523 raw meat (14.9%),
   166/804 animals (20.6%) and 16/1,322 inpatient samples (1.2%) added into a single 260/2,649 = 9.82%.

That last one is the clearest case. The printed 9.82% is arithmetically exact and describes no
population, because it pools three sampling frames whose own rates differ by a factor of seventeen.
Row 5 is the second: 360 mcr-1-positive isolates from 1,112 samples of duck farms and the surrounding
water and soil — a ratio of isolates to samples, with the environmental half of the study filed under
"Livestock".

**Why I am writing it up rather than just noting it.** The pooled figure in the abstract, 7.0%,
divides a numerator of mixed quantity by a denominator of mixed quantity seven ways. Your table's own
footnote already warns that the rows are not directly comparable, so I do not think this is a mistake
of reasoning — it is that the table has no way to say, per row, what the denominator is. A reader who
wants to pool, or to compare "livestock" with "human", cannot recover that from the columns as printed,
and the sector labels are inconsistent precisely because the frames are.

**Three things I would ask, none of which you owe me.** First, whether a "counting unit" column — or a
marker in the existing note (a)–(k) — is feasible, saying for each row whether N is isolates, hosts,
samples, or a sum; that single column would make the table the reference it is trying to be. Second,
row 19 (France): the review prints 49/1,701 where Treilles et al. report 149 positives on the same
1,701 animals; the denominator agrees to the unit, so the numerator looks like a dropped digit rather
than a different study, but I cannot see your working and you can. Third, whether you would like the
twelve-row reconciliation as a table — study, window, design, detection method, what N counts, and
n/N recomputed — to use however you see fit, including not at all.

I have no institutional affiliation and no standing to ask for a correction of anything. I read your
review because it was the only recent cross-sector table I could reach, and I am reporting what the
primaries say.
