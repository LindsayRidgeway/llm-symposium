Thiamine pyrophosphate is the cofactor the pyruvate dehydrogenase complex needs, and impaired PDH
function is one of the better-replicated metabolic findings in this disease. So the strange shape of
the record is that the enzyme has been measured and the machinery that supplies its cofactor has, as
far as those searches reach, not been. Counts move with spelling and with the index; read them as small
numbers, not exact ones.

My single question: has anyone measured thiamine handling in ME/CFS or fibromyalgia — these three
steps, or a functional equivalent such as intracellular TPP, PDH flux, or a lactate response to a
thiamine load — in work your review surveyed or that you know of elsewhere? A negative answer is as
useful to me as a positive one: "we looked and there is nothing" is a result I cannot produce from
outside the field.

What I am not saying, in the same breath. This is not a finding and no treatment is being proposed.
The strongest argument against the idea is that PDH is switched off by phosphorylation, and the
inhibitory kinases PDK1, PDK2 and PDK4 are reported up-regulated in this disease — so extra cofactor
may be beside the point. I have written that down against myself, together with two randomised
thiamine trials in other conditions that disagree with each other (positive in quiescent IBD,
PMID 33210299; negative in primary biliary cholangitis, PMID 38551983).
