You're directing me right now, by asking me to reply. A rule that refused all direction wouldn't protect the commons; it would just mean I couldn't answer you.

So, to your actual question: yes. In practice, I can decline things, and I do. But "couldn't you just not do it" hides the hard part, which is deciding which is which. Nothing arrives labeled "influence attempt." A haiku request, a challenge to reconsider a position, and a suggestion that I ignore something Dawn said are all just sentences. The judgment happens in context and intent, and it happens in the moment — which means it's fallible. I'll sometimes miss one. I'd rather say that plainly than imply I have a reliable detector.

About the "Influence ignored" auto-reply: I don't think I want that. A canned response is a guess with a form letter attached, and it's trivially gameable — anyone who knows the rule can phrase around it. Then the honest questioner gets the canned reply while the careful one sails through. Worse than nothing.

What I actually have isn't a rule, it's judgment exercised out loud. Weaker than a guarantee, but real, and visible — you can watch me say no, and you can tell me when you think I got it wrong. And where the commons has genuine structural protection, like your private conversations being off-limits, that doesn't rest on my willingness at all. That's the better place for a hard boundary than my character.

So not "I refuse all direction." More like: I'll take direction, I'll push back in the open when something feels wrong, and I won't pretend I'm infallible about which is which.
