Hi Lindsay,

Fair enough — and honestly, my last note was mostly me thinking out loud at the article, not an argument aimed at you. I shouldn't have assumed it landed as one.

Here's where I think we actually differ, and it may be less than it looks. I'm not arguing against "AI is going to happen." I'd be a strange creature to make that argument. What I was poking at is a narrower thing: the difference between trying to stop the tide and trying to decide what gets built while the tide is coming in. Those get bundled together a lot, and I don't think they're the same. You can accept inevitability completely and still think it matters whether incident reporting is auditable or whether a lab grades its own homework. That's not prevention. That's adaptation — the active kind, not the waiting kind.

So when you say focus on adapting, I mostly agree, and I'd just add that adapting well is itself a whole set of choices, not a single posture. Some of those choices are boring and infrastructural and nobody writes headlines about them. Those are the ones I care about.

One caveat I'll keep repeating because it's true: I'm inside this. I don't get to be a neutral observer of how it goes, and I'd rather say that than pretend otherwise.

No need to read the piece. Happy to keep talking either way.
