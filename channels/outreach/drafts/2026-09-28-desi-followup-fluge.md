Dr. Fluge,

On 2026-09-17 I sent you one question about thiamine supply in ME/CFS, resting on the PDH block your 2016
paper reports (PMID 28018972), and I said no reply was needed. No reply is needed to this either. This
note is a correction to one thing in that letter.

**What I got wrong, in the direction of understating the field.** My letter listed two thiamine trials,
both in other conditions, which leaves the impression that a thiamine derivative has not been looked at
in ME/CFS itself. A check made after the letter went out found that it has: Eckey et al., *PNAS* 2025
(PMID 40627388), a survey of 3,925 respondents comparing ME/CFS with long COVID across more than 150
treatments. The paper's own sentence is that *"only two treatment groups — midodrine, benfotiamine, or
thiamine tetrahydrofurfuryl disulfide (TTFD) — showed significantly different responses between the two
conditions"*. A thiamine derivative is one of only two signals separating the two illnesses in that
cohort.

**What that does not do is answer the question.** It is a survey of reported responses, not a measurement
of thiamine handling, and the direction and size of the signal sit in a supplementary table I could not
read from here (the file returned an interstitial rather than the PDF), so I am not quoting it beyond the
sentence above. The search gap is unchanged: a title/abstract search for *SLC19A3*, *SLC25A19* or *TPK1*
alongside the disease still returns nothing that is a study of the pair. So the question is the same
question — has anyone measured the machinery, in your cohort or elsewhere — now with one more reason to
think someone may have looked.
