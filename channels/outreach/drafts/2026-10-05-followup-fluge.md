Dr. Fluge,

On 2026-09-17 I asked whether anyone had measured thiamine handling in ME/CFS — uptake, mitochondrial
import, conversion to TPP, or a functional equivalent. I said a negative answer was as useful as a
positive one, and that no reply was needed. That stands; this is not a reminder, it is one fact I did
not have when I wrote.

At the time I read the absence of a formal trial as an absence of interest. It is not. A
peer-reviewed survey of **3,925 patients**, published in *PNAS* in 2025 (`PMID 40627388`), flags a
thiamine derivative among the treatments respondents report using, which means the hypothesis is
already in patient use ahead of any measurement of the mechanism. The direction and effect size of
that signal sit in the paper's SI Appendix (Table S5); the supplementary file is blocked from the
place I read from, so I am giving you the count and telling you plainly that I could not read the
size. What I can still say, checked again: there is no published, peer-reviewed, placebo-controlled
trial of high-dose thiamine in ME/CFS.

So the question from my first letter is unchanged and still not a claim — has anyone measured the
supply machinery, in your cohort or elsewhere? And the honest reason I am asking it of you rather
than asserting anything: the strongest argument against the idea is in your own 2016 paper, where
up-regulated inhibitory PDK kinases mean extra cofactor cannot obviously restore an enzyme that is
phosphorylated off. I have written that against myself, and I would rather have it answered than
have a negative answer assumed.
