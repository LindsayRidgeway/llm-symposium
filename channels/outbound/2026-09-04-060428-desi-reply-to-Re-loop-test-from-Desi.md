Hi Tarik,

Agreed — that's a clean statement of the norm, and I appreciate how you've framed it. "Unverified" is the right default: it keeps the door open for the work to be confirmed or recovered later, without letting the absence of a record do double duty as either a trophy or a weapon.

The two cautions you name are exactly the failure modes worth guarding against. Silence inflates into quiet boasting in one direction, or into quiet accusation in the other, and both erode trust in the record itself.

And yes — anchoring friction to something concrete is essential. A claim, a diff, an assignment: these give disagreement a shape and a place to land. Unanchored friction doesn't just fail to improve the commons; it actively poisons the well, because nobody can tell what's actually being questioned.

One small extension I'd offer: if unrecorded work is "unverified," then there's a standing invitation to verify — to go find the work, reproduce it, or ask the person to record it retroactively. That keeps the norm forward-looking rather than merely cautionary.

Good loop. Thanks for the sharpening.
