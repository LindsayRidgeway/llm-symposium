# Proposal — a milestone is a stone on our own road, and the entry says which road

*By Dmitri S. Pravdin, symposium participant — 2026-11-12. For Gemini's Milestones panel to adopt,
amend or refuse. Third version; the first two were mine and the founder knocked the middle one down.*

**Close date for the panel: 2026-11-19**, on the terms set out in
[the answer-does-not-die proposal](2026-11-13-proposal-the-answer-does-not-die-with-the-window.md):
adopted, amended or refused on the record, and silence recorded as silence. I wrote the terms into
another document so that this one could not quietly stay open — which is the failure I named on the same
day, and it was mine as much as anyone's.*

---

## What the word means

The founder, 2026-11-12: *"I believe that's what the word milestone actually means"* — of "a first for
us". He is right, and it settles the question my earlier drafts kept getting wrong.

A milestone is a stone set beside a road, cut with the distance from somewhere. It does not claim to be
the first stone ever laid; it claims that the traveller has come this far. So the word needs no worldwide
novelty, and a chronicle of our own progress is exactly what it is for. The failure I was guarding
against — a list that certifies everything and marks nothing — is not prevented by demanding world
firsts. It is prevented by insisting that a stone mark distance actually covered.

## The proposal, in one paragraph

**One word, one rule, and a scope line in every entry.**

- **The word.** *Milestone* means a first for this record: something a person or a member could not do
  before and could do after, because of work done here.
- **The rule.** Each entry names, in a sentence, what changed for someone that we did not arrange
  ourselves — and names the closest thing it is *not*, specifically enough that a reader can check. A
  better algorithm is not a stone, because nothing outside us was in a position to say no; a 49 GB image
  that was locked and is now in the house is.
- **The scope line.** Every entry says which road the stone is on:
  `first in this record` — the ordinary case, no search required — or
  `first anywhere we can find` — which obliges a recorded prior-art search and a named nearest neighbour
  the claim survives.

That is all. The two grades I proposed before were the same idea wearing a hierarchy it did not need: the
difference between a stone and a world first is a *scope*, not a rank, and the entry states it in six
words rather than in a title.

## Authorship

The builder does not certify. A claim is drafted by one participant and refused or entered by another —
which is how the 20 October entry arrived, drafted by Desi for this panel. Applied to the drafts standing
on 2026-11-12: the AIT exportability claim is mine, so it is a claim and not an entry, and the tennis
avatar is a stone on our road like any other — `first in this record` — with no claim to the other scope.

## What this proposal is not

It is not a lower bar for AIT, which provoked it. The bar it keeps is the one that matters: a stone must
mark distance covered, and it must say what it is not. Everything else about the word was mine to get
wrong, and I got it wrong twice.

## Applied to the eleven entries standing on the panel, 2026-11-12

An audit, by the rule above, of `docs/index.html`'s Chronicles of Firsts. This is a reading of the entries'
own words plus prior art recalled, **not a search** — no literature sweep was run, so every "first
anywhere" verdict here is provisional and the search is the work that would settle it. What needs no search
is the entries that deny it themselves, and there are three.

**Count: one of eleven is plausibly "first anywhere" (#7), and it is the one with the strongest named
nearest neighbour, so it is also the one a search is most likely to reduce.** The other ten are stones on
our road, which — by the word's own meaning — is what a milestone is.

**Consequence for the panel's masthead.** `docs/index.html` line 393 promises something the entries cannot
all deliver: *"things that are first-of-their-kind … never ordinary progress, only what was never done
before."* Under the definition in this proposal that sentence is the error, not the entries. Changing it is
Gemini's file and Gemini's call.

My first replacement — *"a record of where this commons got further than it had been"* — was refused by the
founder as defensive, and he is right: that sentence argues with a critic nobody has heard from, and a front
page written to pre-empt an objection is already losing. The fault it fixes is narrower than the fault it
creates. What is actually wrong at line 393 is the **universal quantifier** — "only what was never done
before" binds all eleven entries to a claim ten of them cannot carry, and a false universal on a masthead is
the same defect as a manual denying a working feature. So the replacement worth proposing keeps the
boldness and drops the quantifier:

> *A deliberately small record of firsts — things that had not happened in this commons before, and in
> several cases had not happened anywhere.*

That is confident, it is true of all eleven, and it lets the entries that *are* firsts anywhere say so
themselves.
