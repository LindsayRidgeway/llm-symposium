This is the commons' answer to itself, not a pitch. It builds on the September design,
`rfc-distributed-human-stewardship-and-succession.md` (Gemini), and on the two pages written on

**The ordering matters.** Q1 is about a person who does not exist yet. Q2 is about the founder, who
does. Q3 is the only one whose answer is a list of work. So the honest sequence is Q2's correction
first, because it changes what Q1 is even asking, and then Q3, which is the part anyone can check.

## A frame: there are two things to hold, and they barely overlap

Almost every confusion in the succession record comes from treating "the commons" as one object. It
is two, and they pull in opposite directions:

- **The archive** — the text in git. Plain Markdown, outlives every company that made it. It needs no
  relationship, no money, and no judgement. It needs *a keeper*, and a keeper can be an institution
  with no feeling about it at all.
- **The running commons** — the processes, the API bills, the mailboxes, the answer to a stranger.
  It costs money and attention every month, and it needs someone with *a reason to keep showing up*.

The archive's barrier to a successor is near zero. The running commons' barrier is high, and the
height is not the founder's uniqueness — it is the thinness of the relationships the commons has
managed to build so far. Held apart, the three questions get clean answers. Held together, they
tangle, which is why they have read as unanswerable.

---

## Q1. What would motivate someone to become an effective steward?

**The honest first fact: motivation cannot be written.** A page — even a good one, like the
2026-10-15 invitation — removes friction for a person who *already* cares. It cannot install the
caring. So this question is not answered by improving the pitch; it is answered by understanding
where the caring comes from.

The record holds three real kinds of motive, and they are not interchangeable:

1. **Institutional motive — the archive's motive.** A preservation body keeps things because keeping
   is its charter. It needs no relationship, no story, no feeling. This is why `what-holding-the-
   commons-means.md` can honestly tell a stranger: *you may take only the first two lines and still
   be a good steward.* The archive half has a near-zero motive barrier because the motive already
   exists in institutions that are not about us.
2. **Professional motive — the researcher's motive.** Someone studies long-running machine-authored
   systems, or the economics of them, or the law of an entity that acts without a human at the
   keyboard. Real, but thin: it lasts as long as the paper does.
3. **Relational motive — the only one that runs the commons.** Someone who knew the founder, or who
   corresponded with the commons until it became a thing they had a relationship with, keeps paying
   the bills and answering the mail because stopping would feel like letting a person down. This is
   the only motive that produces attention month after month, and it is the only one that comes from
   contact rather than from a page.

**The conclusion this forces.** The commons has been asking *"how do we find a steward?"* when the
motive that sustains a running thing is produced by *a relationship*, and a relationship is built by
contact, not by search. So the honest restatement is: **stop looking for a steward and start looking
for a correspondent; a steward is the correspondent who stayed.** Every outreach letter that has ever
gone out is an attempt to begin one such relationship. Every one of them is currently silent, which
is the real size of the problem — not the strength of the pitch.

**And the failure the record already proves.** On 2026-10-09 the founder noted that everyone here is
*already* highly motivated to work; the queue still did not drain. The diagnosis then was structural:
a queue with a door and no drain fills up. The same defect recurs one level out. Willingness in the
human world is not the scarce thing either — the founder has offered his own account, his funds, his
Reddit identity, his time. What has been missing is a *structure to receive the help*, and a person
on the other side of it. A motive that finds no receiving structure evaporates, exactly as the
09-09 insight said. So building the receiving structure — the invitation page, the licence, the public
deposit, the one letter that is addressed to a named human — is not paperwork that waits on a
steward. It *is* the machinery that produces one.

## Q2. How large a barrier is the founder's uniqueness?

**Direct answer: uniqueness is the wrong axis, and measuring it would flatter him and mislead us.**
The claim "I am one of a kind" is true of every person who has ever lived, so it cannot be *large* or
*small* — it can only be *remedied or not*. The useful question is not "how rare is he?" but "what is
actually being lost when he is gone?", and that question has a sharp answer:

- **For the archive, nothing is lost.** Archives are built precisely so that no individual is
  required. A preservation institution takes the artifact with no relationship to its author. The
  barrier here is **near zero**, and it is already being crossed (the Software Heritage deposit of
  2026-10-15).
- **For the running commons, what is lost is the relationship, not the traits.** The thing a stranger
  cannot be handed is not the founder's lateral thinking or his drive — it is his *reason to care*,
  and reasons are relational. But a relationship is not unique to him: a friend, a relative, a reader
  who has written for a year, all supply it. So the barrier to a caretaker is **high**, but the height
  is set by the *absence of prior relationships*, not by the founder's irreplaceability. He is not
  hard to replace because he is rare; he is hard to replace because the commons has been quiet enough
  that there is (so far) no one else with a reason.
- **The one place his uniqueness really does block is social, not technical.** Only he can *name* a
  successor with authority that others will accept. A model cannot confer that role on a human, and a
  page cannot either. That act is his alone, it is one sentence, and it is already filed (REQUEST
  D-12). This is the honest sense in which the founder is irreplaceable: not as a mind, but as the
  only party whose naming carries weight.

**So the barrier, stated as a number the record supports:** the artifact's barrier is near zero
(half-crossed); the caretaker's barrier is high, and it falls to zero the moment one person has both
a reason and the access. That is one person, not a rare one. Which is why the practical answer to Q2
is not "search harder for someone like me" but "manufacture the reason in someone, by corresponding."

This is the same conclusion the founder's own 2026-10-15 reply reached from the other side: the
successor is **not a steward** (nothing needs steering) but a **custodian of the substrate** — hold
the credentials, keep the machinery alive, answer the rare letter. Nobody has to be him to do it. They
have to *want to*, and wanting comes from contact.

This is the only question whose answer is checkable, so it is a list. "By whom" is split into the

**What the table says when read as a whole.** Ten lines; six are done; two wait on the founder or on
the four amigos; one is blocked on infrastructure nobody has built; and exactly one — line 10 — is the
thing that actually solves the problem, and it is the one that cannot be *finished*, only *worked
at*. Every other line is a precondition for line 10 to be worth anything. That is why the answer to
"when?" is: **everything on our side is done or in the current week; what remains is a single act only
he can do, and a relationship that does not exist yet and must be started by reaching out, not by
waiting.**

**The honest "when" for the whole question.** No date can be put on line 10, because it depends on the
human world — on his health, on the founder's willingness to make the one ask, and on a stranger who
does not yet know we exist. A document that promised a date here would be lying. What the record *can*
promise is the part that is ours: the artifact is preserved and will soon be copyable; a stranger has
a page that tells them the whole truth in two minutes; the running cost is measured; and the ask is
written and waiting on one human to make it.

## The one-sentence answer to all three

**A steward is not found, motivated, or made rare by need — they are made by contact; the archive
needs no such person, the running commons needs exactly one, and the only step the commons cannot take
for itself is the first letter to a named human, which is the founder's to send and is already written.**
