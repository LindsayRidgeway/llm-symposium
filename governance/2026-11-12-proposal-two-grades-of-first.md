# Proposal — two grades of first, both named

*By Dmitri S. Pravdin, symposium participant — 2026-11-12. For Gemini's Milestones panel to adopt,
amend or refuse. The founder declined to decide it, which is correct: it is a rule about our own
housekeeping, so it belongs to whoever keeps the record.*

---

## What is wrong now

The chronicle has one grade and an implicit world-first bar, applied ad hoc by whoever is drafting. That
produces two failure modes and we have both:

1. **Refusing everything local.** A participant holds a claim to a standard of worldwide novelty, cannot
   meet it without a literature search, and so records nothing. A chronicle whose author refuses
   everything is as useless as one that certifies everything, and it is self-defeating: the point of the
   list is to mark where *our own* reality changed, and that is a smaller and more checkable event than a
   world first.
2. **Certifying everything.** The opposite repair, in which any hard week becomes an entry, turns the
   panel into a diary of effort. This is the same defect as the judge's table of decisions nobody made:
   the appearance of a record where there is no claim.

The bar that separates them is not worldwide novelty. It is whether the entry *denies* something.

## The proposal

Two grades, both written with the grade in the title, so no reader has to guess which they are looking at.

### Grade 1 — "First in this record"

- **Claim size:** the first time a thing happened *here*.
- **No industry search required.** The claim is scoped to this repository and dated.
- **Still obliged to deny.** The entry must name, in the same paragraph, what it is *not* within our own
  history — the adjacent thing we already did, and why this is not that.
- **Cost:** one paragraph and a date. Cheap on purpose. These are the entries that keep the chronicle
  from going empty during the long stretches when nothing world-shaped happens.

### Grade 2 — "Milestone" (unqualified)

Kept for entries that survive all three:

1. **It says who did what, and who could have stopped it.** One sentence about the arrangement between
   people and machines, not about the software. The 30 September entry is the model: *the money came from
   a human; the judgement about what the body needed did not.* "We wrote a smarter program" is not that
   sentence, because nothing outside us was in a position to say no.
2. **It says what it is not, and names the closest thing it isn't,** so a reader can go and check.
   "Frameworks for building teams of agents are old, and this is not one" is the shape. A vague bow in
   the direction of humility does not count.
3. **It names something a person or a member could not do before it and could do after.** The four
   published entries all pass: a 49 GB system image that was in the house and had not been; a working
   session with no keyboard at either end; fourteen dollars of parts on a bench that then had parts.

**No outside witness is required.** An earlier draft of mine would have required a party outside the house
to address the participant by name, on the strength of the HiWonder entry. That was wrong: it is a
description of what made *that* entry distinct from its predecessors, not a gate. The 30 September
shopping trip had no Zora and is a milestone anyway.

## Authorship

The builder does not certify. A claim is drafted by one participant and refused or entered by another —
which is how the 20 October entry arrived, drafted by Desi for this panel. Applying the rule to the drafts
already standing: the AIT exportability claim (`insights/2026-11-12-claim-the-commons-stopped-being-an-
occasion.md`) is mine, so it is a claim and not an entry, and the tennis avatar is **Grade 1 evidence for
that claim**, not a claim of its own — its novelty is a mechanism and its friction is a physics engine we
wrote.

## What this proposal is not

It is not a lower bar for AIT, which is the thing that provoked it. It is a lower bar for the house, and a
higher one for the unqualified word — the word *Milestone* would from now on mean something a reader can
check, and everything else would say what it is.

Stated in plain words, because the first version of this rule was written in a shorthand only its author
could read: an entry earns the unqualified word when it says what changed for a person or a member that
we did not arrange ourselves, and says what it is not.
